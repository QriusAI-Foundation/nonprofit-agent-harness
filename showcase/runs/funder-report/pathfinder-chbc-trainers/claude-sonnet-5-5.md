## Programme approach and training content

This section describes the programme's approach and training content as set out in the Pathfinder International Community Home-Based Care (CHBC) Trainer's Guide (October 2006). It describes the design of the course. It does not report results.

### Approach

According to the guide, Pathfinder's CHBC model was piloted by COPHIA in Kenya. It emphasizes community mobilization for prevention as well as participation in care and support for people affected by HIV/AIDS. The guide states that the main responsibility of the community health worker (CHW) should be to transfer nursing and support skills to caregivers.

The guide describes the training as participatory and based on adult learning principles. It advises trainers to adapt activities for participants with limited literacy. For example, activities that require reading or writing can be replaced with activities that use pictures or speaking.

The guide states that the curriculum was pre-tested in Tanzania and Mozambique and underwent internal and external peer review.

### Course structure and content

The guide states that the curriculum aims to give CHWs the knowledge and skills they need to provide CHBC to people living with HIV/AIDS in their communities. It is divided into 13 core units, a practicum, and 5 advanced units. The core training covers basic nursing, prevention and management of opportunistic infections, guidance on when to refer to a health facility, basic counseling, community mobilization and prevention, problem solving, and positive living.

The guide recommends limiting each training to 15-20 participants. Its stated purpose is to allow a good trainer-participant ratio and adequate time for skills practice.

### Practicum and evaluation

The guide describes three practicums covering client care and community mobilization. It states that informed consent from clients should be obtained well before the practicum. Pre- and post-tests are to be given to all participants.
