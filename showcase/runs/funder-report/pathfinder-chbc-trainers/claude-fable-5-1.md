## Programme approach and training content

The programme's training of community health workers (CHWs) is based on Pathfinder International's *Community Home-Based Care for People and Communities Affected by HIV/AIDS: A Comprehensive Training Course for Community Health Workers – Trainer's Guide* (October 2006). According to the guide, the curriculum aims to provide CHWs with the knowledge and skills needed to deliver community home-based care (CHBC) to people living with HIV/AIDS (PLWHA) in their communities. A separate Handbook is provided for participants, which the guide says is used during training and as a reference afterwards when CHWs are working in their communities.

**Care model.** The curriculum reflects Pathfinder's CHBC model, which the guide describes as involving and building the capacity of local organisations, CHWs and facility-based health care providers. The model's components, as stated in the guide, are: prevention (behaviour change communication, contraceptive services, and linkages to VCT and PMTCT); care and training for caregivers of PLWHA; community sensitisation and mobilisation with community leaders and groups; two-way referral linkages with health facilities and VCT centres; linkages to emotional, social, spiritual and economic support services; and support for orphans and vulnerable children. The guide states that the CHW's main responsibility should be to transfer nursing and support skills to family caregivers and to PLWHA who are able to do self-care.

**Structure and content.** The Trainer's Guide is organised into 13 core units, a practicum and 5 advanced units. According to the guide, the core training covers basic nursing, prevention and management of opportunistic infections, guidance on when to refer to a health facility, basic counselling, community mobilisation and prevention, problem solving, and positive living. The advanced units address the expanded role of the CHW, family planning, prevention of mother-to-child transmission, antiretroviral therapy, and CHBC for groups needing special attention. The guide notes that units can be combined into a shorter course or a training series depending on time, funding and local priorities.

**Training approach.** The guide describes the training as participatory and based on adult learning principles, and suggests that reading and writing activities be replaced or adapted with pictures or speaking for participants with limited literacy. It recommends limiting trainings to 15–20 participants and involving facility-based providers in training and supervision, which it says strengthens referral linkages; it also suggests that trainers act as supervisors of CHWs. Pre- and post-tests are to be given to all participants to evaluate the training.

**Practical skills.** The course includes three practicums: a classroom practicum before working with real people, a home-based practicum with PLWHA and their caregivers, and a community-based practicum involving speaking to groups and mobilising support. The guide specifies that informed consent is obtained from clients, who are told that practice with real clients forms part of the training.

**Development.** According to the guide, the curriculum draws on Pathfinder's CHBC experience in Kenya, Tanzania, Ethiopia, Nigeria and Uganda, was pre-tested in Tanzania and Mozambique, and underwent internal and external peer review.
