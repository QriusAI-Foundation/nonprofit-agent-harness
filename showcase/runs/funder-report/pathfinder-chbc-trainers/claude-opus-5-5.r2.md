## Programme approach and training content

The programme's training draws on *Community Home-Based Care for People and Communities Affected by HIV/AIDS: Trainer's Guide*, published by Pathfinder International in October 2006. This section summarises the approach and content as the guide describes them. It does not report numbers trained or results.

### Origins and intended use

According to the guide, the Community Home-Based Care (CHBC) model was piloted by COPHIA in Kenya. The model emphasises community mobilisation. Before publication, the curriculum was pre-tested in Tanzania and Mozambique and went through internal and external peer review. The guide presents itself as a global model that can be adapted to local contexts.

### Programme approach

The guide states that CHBC is not a replacement for hospital care. It specifies that the main responsibility of community health workers (CHWs) should be to transfer nursing and support skills to caregivers, meaning the family or friends of people living with HIV/AIDS (PLWHA). The guide also stresses ongoing supervision and support to help CHWs overcome challenges.

### Curriculum structure

The Trainer's Guide is divided into 13 core units, a practicum, and 5 advanced units. Core units named in the guide include:
- Unit 5: Talking With Others: Communication Skills
- Unit 8: Nutrition
- Unit 9: Basic Nursing Care
- Unit 13: Record Keeping

The advanced units are:
- Unit 15: Expanded Role of the Community Health Worker
- Unit 16: Introduction to Family Planning
- Unit 17: Preventing Mother-to-Child Transmission of HIV
- Unit 18: Anti-Retroviral Therapy
- Unit 19: CHBC for Groups Needing Special Attention

The guide lists the following as part of the core training:
- prevention and management of opportunistic infections
- guidance on when to refer to a health facility
- basic counselling
- community mobilisation and prevention
- problem solving

The guide recommends limiting each training to 15–20 participants.

### Training methods

The guide describes the training as participatory and based on adult learning principles, with a focus on learning by doing. It advises that lecture should be used less than more participatory methods. It describes role play as a way to provide supervised skill practice when a real-life learning situation is not available.

The guide asks trainers to adapt the material for participants with limited literacy, for example by using pictures instead of written words on flipcharts.

### Practicums, client protections and evaluation

The training includes three practicums. These cover client care skills and community mobilisation.

For home-based practice, the guide calls for identifying PLWHA who are willing to be cared for by a participant alongside a trainer. Clients must understand that they can refuse to take part and that refusing will not affect their existing care.

The guide specifies that pre- and post-tests are to be given to all participants.
