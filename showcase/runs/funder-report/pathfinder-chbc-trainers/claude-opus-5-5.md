## Programme approach and training content

The programme's training material is the Trainer's Guide *Community Home-Based Care for People and Communities Affected by HIV/AIDS: A Comprehensive Training Course for Community Health Workers*, published by Pathfinder International in October 2006.

### Purpose and basis

The guide states that the course goal is to add skilled community health workers (CHWs) to the forces fighting the HIV/AIDS epidemic. The curriculum aims to give CHWs the knowledge and skills needed to provide community home-based care (CHBC) to people living with HIV/AIDS (PLWHA) in their communities.

The curriculum draws on Pathfinder staff experience in implementing a CHBC model in Kenya, Tanzania, Ethiopia, Nigeria and Uganda. According to the guide, it was pre-tested in Tanzania and Mozambique and underwent internal and external peer review.

### Structure

The materials comprise a Trainer's Guide for trainers and a Handbook for participants. The Trainer's Guide is organised into 13 core units, a practicum, and 5 advanced units.

The guide describes the curriculum as flexible. Units can be combined into a shorter course or a training series, depending on the time and funding available and on local priorities.

### Content

The guide lists the core training as covering:

- basic nursing;
- prevention and management of opportunistic infections;
- guidance on when to refer to a health facility;
- basic counselling;
- community mobilisation and prevention;
- problem solving;
- positive living.

It states that the CHW's main responsibility should be to transfer nursing and support skills to caregivers, and to PLWHA who are able to provide self-care.

### Methods

The guide describes the training as participatory and based on adult learning principles, with a focus on learning by doing. It advises that lectures be used less than more participatory methods. It recommends limiting trainings to 15–20 participants to ensure adequate time for skills practice.

The guide notes that CHWs' experience and literacy can vary greatly. It suggests that reading and writing activities can be replaced or adapted with activities that use pictures or speaking.

The guide includes three practicums covering client care and community mobilisation. The first is held in the classroom, so that skills are practised before participants work with real people.

### Assessment

The guide specifies that pre- and post-tests are to be given to all participants.
