## Programme approach and training content

The CBHFA in action volunteer manual, issued by the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies in March 2009, sets out the training material for the community-based health and first aid programme. The programme is described as dynamic and flexible. Volunteers learn about first aid, health promotion, disease prevention and disaster preparedness in the classroom and then put what they have learnt into action in their own communities, bringing that experience back to share with others. This is summarised as the learning-by-doing method: learn in the classroom, do in the community.

The manual states that CBHFA trains and mobilizes volunteers from the community to carry out activities in the community, on the basis that local volunteers understand better how a particular community lives and works. The programme's stated focus is on bringing first aid for common injuries to the community, identifying and addressing community health priorities, advocating health promotion and disease prevention, and preparing volunteers to respond to disasters.

The training is organised into modules. The first three modules are obligatory and cover the Red Cross Red Crescent movement and volunteer role, community mobilization, and community assessment. The manual states that the remaining modules contain topics on first aid, health promotion and disease prevention, with some topics obligatory and many optional. Module 4 covers basic first aid and injury prevention, including principles of first aid, basic life support, and first aid for choking, bleeding, shock, burns, injuries, poisoning and other conditions. Module 5 covers community mobilization in major emergencies, and Module 6 covers disease prevention and health promotion across topics such as family planning, safe motherhood, newborn care, nutrition, immunization, safe water and sanitation, and malaria. Module 7 covers supplementary topics such as road safety and safe blood.

Each module includes learning objectives, main learning points, a topic summary, practice checklists and check-your-understanding questions. Modules also include Community Tools, which are described as a set of dialogue cue cards used with household and community members. Volunteers practise using these tools in the classroom before taking them into the community.

The manual indicates that the programme is designed to respond to the priority health needs identified by each community. Community assessment in Module 3 is intended to determine which three to five health, first aid and safety priorities a community will address, and the training content is then selected to match those priorities.
