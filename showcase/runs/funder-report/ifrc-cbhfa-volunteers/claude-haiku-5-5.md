## Programme approach and training content

The Community-based health and first aid in action (CBHFA) volunteer manual, published by the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies in March 2009, sets out the training content for volunteers. The programme is described as a learning-by-doing approach: volunteers learn in the classroom and then act in their own communities, bringing that experience back to share with others.

The manual states that CBHFA brings first aid for common injuries to the community, identifies and addresses community health priorities, advocates health promotion and disease prevention, and prepares volunteers to respond to disasters. The programme is described as flexible, with content responding to priority concerns identified by the community itself.

The manual is organised into seven modules. Module 1 covers the Red Cross Red Crescent movement, its components, emblems and seven Fundamental Principles, and the role of the volunteer. Module 2 covers community mobilization, including communication skills, organizing communities, a sensitization meeting and social mobilization with the Community Tools. Module 3 covers community assessment, using secondary information, direct observation, transect walks, community maps, seasonal calendars, focus group discussions and household visits, followed by an action plan. Module 4 covers basic first aid and injury prevention across 20 topics, five of which are optional. Module 5 covers community mobilization in major emergencies and public health in emergencies. Module 6 covers disease prevention and health promotion, including family planning, safe motherhood, newborn care, nutrition, immunization, water and sanitation, diarrhoea, respiratory infections, malaria, HIV and other topics. Module 7 covers supplementary topics: road safety, safe blood and blood donor recruitment, excessive substance use, and collection and burial of the dead.

The manual indicates that the first three modules are obligatory, while many later topics are optional and chosen according to the priorities identified in the community assessment. Each module includes learning objectives, main learning points, topic summaries, practice checklists and check-your-understanding questions.

The manual's own framing is that its content is training material for volunteers. It does not report results or outcomes from implementation, and this section does not describe any.
