## Programme approach and training content

This section describes the programme as set out in the Volunteer Manual for Community-based health and first aid in action (CBHFA), published by the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies in March 2009. It describes the approach and content only. The manual does not report participant numbers or outcomes, so none are given here.

### Approach

The manual describes a method of learning in the classroom and applying that learning in the community. Volunteers are drawn from the communities in which they work. The content they study is meant to respond to priorities the community itself has identified.

### Structure of the training

The first three modules are obligatory and cover the background skills volunteers need to help the community decide its priority concerns. Module 3 includes the identification and prioritization of health, first aid and safety issues. Later modules are chosen in response to those priorities. In Module 6, for example, volunteers choose at least five of 16 topics according to the priorities set in the community assessment. Module 2 includes a sensitization meeting that introduces the programme to the community.

### Training content

- **Module 4 (first aid and injury prevention):** It has 20 topics, five of them optional. The manual advises that psychological first aid be considered alongside all first aid procedures. It also states that the basic life support skills must be taught by qualified first aid trainers recognized by the National Society.
- **Module 5 (emergencies):** It prepares volunteers to be mobilized in disasters and epidemic outbreaks.
- **Module 6 (disease prevention and health promotion):** It uses the Community Tools, a set of dialogue cue cards for households and community members. Volunteers are taught to apply the Ask-Praise-Advise-Check method when using them.
- **Module 7 (supplementary topics):** It covers topics volunteers may wish to learn in order to provide community education and assistance.

## Evidence cited

1. The training is built on a learning-by-doing method in which volunteers learn in the classroom and then apply the learning in the community.
   > "This is the learning by doing method: learn in the classroom, do in the community." (Introduction)
2. Volunteers are recruited from the communities where they live and work.
   > "Red Cross Red Crescent volunteers are recruited from the communities where they live and work." (Module 1, Topic 3: CBHFA in action)
3. What volunteers learn responds to the priority areas of concern that their own community has decided on.
   > "What you learn is in response to what your own community has decided are priority areas of concern." (Introduction)
4. The first three modules are obligatory and provide background skills to help the community decide its priority concerns.
   > "The first three modules of the CBHFA materials are obligatory, and will give you the background skills you need to help your community make decisions about its priority concerns." (Introduction: How CBHFA in action works)
5. Module 3 outcomes include identifying and prioritizing health, first aid and safety issues.
   > "Identification and prioritization of health, first aid and safety issues." (Module 3: Assessment-based action in my community)
6. In Module 6, volunteers choose at least five of 16 topics, depending on the priorities set in the community assessment.
   > "Of the 16 topics in this module, you will have chosen at least five, depending on the priorities you set in the community assessment carried out during Module 3." (Module 6: Disease prevention and health promotion)
7. A sensitization meeting introduces the programme to the community.
   > "A sensitization meeting will introduce CBHFA in action to the community." (Module 2, Topic 3: Sensitizing the community to CBHFA in action)
8. Module 4 has 20 topics, five of which are optional.
   > "There are 20 topics in this module, five of which are optional:" (Module 4: Basic first aid and injury prevention)
9. The manual says psychological first aid should be considered for all first aid procedures.
   > "Psychological first aid should be considered for all first aid procedures." (Module 4, Topic 2: Psychological first aid)
10. The manual states that basic life support skills must be taught by qualified first aid trainers recognized by the National Society.
   > "These skills must be trained by qualified first aid trainers recognized by the National Society." (Module 4, Topic 3: Basic life support (Part 1))
11. Module 5 prepares volunteers to be mobilized in disasters and epidemic outbreaks.
   > "In the event of a disaster or epidemic outbreak, it will be necessary to mobilize as many first responders as possible." (Module 5: Community mobilization in major emergencies)
12. The Community Tools are a set of dialogue cue cards for use with household and community members.
   > "The Community Tools are a set of dialogue cue cards that you can use with household and community members." (Introduction: How CBHFA in action works)
13. Volunteers are taught to apply the Ask-Praise-Advise-Check method when using the Community Tools.
   > "When you use the Community Tools with individuals or communities, you should apply the Ask-Praise-Advise-Check method:" (Module 6, Topic 1: Community health education and promotion)
14. Module 7 covers supplementary topics volunteers may wish to learn to provide community education and assistance.
   > "Module 7 addresses supplementary topics you may wish to learn in order to provide community education and assistance." (Module 7: Supplementary topics)
