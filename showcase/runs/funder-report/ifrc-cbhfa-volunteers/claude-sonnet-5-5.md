## Programme approach and training content

This section describes the programme's approach and training content as set out in the Community-based health and first aid in action (CBHFA) Volunteer Manual, published by the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies in 2009. It describes the design of the training material only. It does not report participation figures or results.

### Approach

The manual describes a learning-by-doing method in which volunteers learn in the classroom and then apply what they have learnt in the community. Volunteers are recruited from the communities in which they live and work. A facilitator supports them and gives feedback on their learning and development.

### Structure of the training

The manual states that the first three modules are obligatory. After that, a community assessment gives the community the means to decide on its priority learning needs, which determines the topics chosen from the remaining modules. Volunteers also use the Community Tools, a set of dialogue cue cards for work with household and community members.

### Module content

- **Module 3 (assessment-based action):** The topics include assessment tools such as direct observation, the transect walk, the community map and the seasonal calendar.
- **Module 4 (first aid):** Volunteers learn to assess, plan, implement and evaluate first aid for various injuries and illnesses. The module has 20 topics, five of which are optional.
- **Module 5 (emergencies):** The module covers major emergencies that may affect the community.
- **Module 6 (disease prevention and health promotion):** Volunteers choose at least five of its 16 topics, depending on the priorities set in the community assessment.
- **Module 7 (supplementary topics):** The topics include road safety.

### Note on use

This description reflects the content of the training material. It is not evidence of how the material was delivered or what it achieved.
