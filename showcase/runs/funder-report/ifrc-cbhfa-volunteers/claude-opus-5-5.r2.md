## Programme approach and training content

The programme's training material is the *Volunteer Manual for Community-based health and first aid in action (CBHFA)*, dated March 2009 and issued by the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies. This section summarises the approach and content the manual sets out. It does not report results.

### Approach

The manual's foreword describes CBHFA as a move towards a broader and more comprehensive approach to injury and disease prevention and health promotion. Under this approach, volunteers drawn from a community are trained and mobilized to carry out activities in that same community. The manual names community participation as the core of CBHFA.

Training follows what the manual calls the learning-by-doing method: volunteers learn in the classroom and then act in the community. The manual describes the programme as flexible. A facilitator supports volunteers and gives feedback on their learning and development.

### Training structure

The first three modules are obligatory. In the later modules, some topics are obligatory, but many are optional.

- **Community mobilization (Module 2).** This module includes a sensitization meeting that introduces CBHFA in action to the community.
- **Community assessment (Module 3).** Volunteers carry out an assessment with community members to identify health, first aid and safety priorities. Assessment tools include:
  - direct observation
  - transect walks
  - community maps
  - seasonal calendars

  Three to five priorities that community volunteer activities can address are then selected for CBHFA activities. Volunteers are asked to complete a report form after each household visit or community activity.
- **First aid (Module 4).** Volunteers learn to assess, plan, implement and evaluate first aid for a range of injuries and illnesses. The module has 20 topics, five of them optional.
- **Emergencies (Module 5).** This module covers major emergencies that may affect the community and how to prepare for and respond to them.
- **Disease prevention and health promotion (Module 6).** The module has 16 topics. Volunteers choose at least five, depending on the priorities set in the Module 3 assessment.
- **Supplementary topics (Module 7).** This module has four topics, including road safety.

### Materials

Volunteers take the Volunteer Manual home with them. They also receive the Community Tools, a set of dialogue cue cards for use with households and community members. Volunteers practise using the Community Tools in the classroom before taking them into the community.
