## Programme approach and training content

The programme's training material is the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies' *Volunteer Manual for Community-based health and first aid in action (CBHFA)*, dated March 2009.

### Approach

The manual's foreword states that CBHFA trains and mobilizes volunteers from the community to carry out activities in that community. Community participation is described as the core of CBHFA. The manual calls its training model the learning by doing method: volunteers learn in the classroom and then act in the community. It also describes the programme as flexible. A facilitator supports volunteers and gives them feedback on their learning.

### Structure of the training content

The first three modules are obligatory. They give volunteers the background skills to help their community make decisions about its priority concerns. In the later modules, some topics are obligatory and many are optional. The community assessment gives the community the means to decide which learning needs are the priority.

- **Module 1** covers volunteers' role and responsibilities within their National Society.
- **Module 2** addresses community mobilization. This includes a sensitization meeting that introduces CBHFA to the community.
- **Module 3** guides the community assessment. Volunteers learn to draw community maps and seasonal calendars. Three to five health and safety priorities are then selected for CBHFA activities. An action plan is developed with the community health committee and local branch staff. A report form is to be completed after each household visit or community activity.
- **Module 4** teaches volunteers to assess, plan, implement and evaluate first aid for injuries and illnesses. It contains 20 topics, five of them optional. Basic life support skills must be taught by qualified first aid trainers recognized by the National Society.
- **Module 5** covers preparing for and responding to major emergencies. It includes a topic on public health in emergencies and epidemics.
- **Module 6** contains 16 disease prevention and health promotion topics. Volunteers choose at least five of these, based on the priorities set in the Module 3 assessment. The module includes the peer education approach.
- **Module 7** offers four supplementary topics. Road safety is one of them.

### Supporting materials

Volunteers take the Volunteer Manual home with them. The Community Tools are a set of dialogue cue cards for use with household and community members.
