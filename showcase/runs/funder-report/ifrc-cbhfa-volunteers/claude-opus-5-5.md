## Programme approach and training content

The programme's training draws on the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies' *Volunteer Manual for Community-based health and first aid in action (CBHFA)*, dated March 2009. The description below reflects what the manual states. It does not report results from our own delivery.

### Approach

The manual describes an integrated model that trains and mobilizes volunteers drawn from the community to carry out activities in that same community. Its teaching method is summarized as "learning by doing": volunteers learn in the classroom and then apply that learning in the community. The manual states that a facilitator supports volunteers and gives them feedback on their learning and development. It describes the programme as flexible, with learning content shaped by priorities that the community itself identifies.

### Structure of the training

According to the manual, the first three modules are obligatory. Among the remaining modules, some topics are obligatory and many are optional.

- **Community engagement (Module 2):** Includes a sensitization meeting that introduces CBHFA to the community.
- **Community assessment and planning (Module 3):** The community assessment gives the community the means to decide on volunteers' priority learning needs. Three to five health and safety priorities are selected for CBHFA activities. The manual states that the action plan should be developed with the community health committee and local branch staff.
- **First aid and injury prevention (Module 4):** Trains volunteers to assess, plan, implement and evaluate first aid for various injuries and illnesses. The module has 20 topics, five of which are optional. Basic life support skills must be taught by qualified first aid trainers recognized by the National Society.
- **Major emergencies (Module 5):** Covers major emergencies that may affect the community and how to act on them.
- **Disease prevention and health promotion (Module 6):** Contains 16 topics. Volunteers choose at least five, based on the priorities set during the community assessment.
- **Supplementary topics (Module 7):** Contains four topics.

### Materials and reporting

The Volunteer Manual is intended for volunteers to take home. The Community Tools are a set of dialogue cue cards for use with household and community members. Volunteers practise using these cards in the classroom before taking them into the community. The manual also states that a report form should be completed after each household visit or whenever CBHFA activities are carried out.
