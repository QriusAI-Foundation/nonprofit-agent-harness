> *Drafting note: the supplied document is not programme training material. It is the World Bank's 2017 impact evaluation of Vietnam Escuela Nueva (VNEN), an external study. This draft therefore describes the VNEN approach and its teacher training as that study reports them. It should not be read as a description of our own curriculum, and it makes no claims about our reach or results.*

## Programme approach and training content

### The VNEN model

According to the World Bank evaluation, VNEN is a "whole school" reform that combines six practices:

- participative and collaborative learning
- self-paced learning guides
- student government
- formative assessment combined with summative assessment
- real-life oriented learning with community integration
- teacher professional networks

The evaluation reports that the GPE-VNEN project and the wider programme sought to implement this approach in 1,447 schools from 2012 to 2016.

### Learning guides and classroom practice

Under the model, learning guides replace textbooks. Each guide is designed to serve as a textbook, an exercise book for students and a teaching guide for teachers. Guide activities follow a set sequence:

1. prior knowledge
2. introduction of a new concept
3. application in known situations
4. application in unknown or future situations

The study notes that group work and learning guides go together, and that teaching in groups without the guides would be difficult. It also states that in a VNEN class each learning activity typically begins with a physical warm-up.

### Assessment, student government and community links

The model stresses formative rather than summative assessment, with the accent on oral and written feedback rather than repetitive testing. One method is for students to check each other's work in pairs. The report describes student government as "learning by doing."

Community integration is built into classroom activities. Each VNEN classroom is supposed to make a community map showing every student's home and major landmarks. Parents are encouraged to teach skills such as local dances, music and weaving.

### Teacher training

The evaluation describes the training as follows:

- All primary teachers in Vietnam receive two weeks of summer training. For most teachers, this was their initial VNEN training.
- Teachers received all training materials at least two weeks before the training.
- The training was run as a VNEN classroom, complete with groups, group leaders and warm-up games.
- A few school teachers took part in national-level training. Multiple feedback loops were then intended to keep the training grounded in reality and to reduce the loss of signal through successive rounds.

The programme also included provisions for professional development throughout the school year through biweekly cluster meetings. The study reports that these meetings were only partly implemented. Teachers in VNEN schools reported an average of three to four cluster meetings per year, compared with roughly 15 or 16 if meetings had been held every two weeks.
