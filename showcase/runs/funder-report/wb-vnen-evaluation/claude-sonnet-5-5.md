## Programme approach and training content

This section draws on the World Bank's 2017 impact evaluation of Vietnam Escuela Nueva (VNEN). It describes the programme's design and teacher training as the study reports them. It does not assess results.

### Programme approach

The study describes VNEN as combining several practices. It says the combination is intended to produce "a transformative and powerful learning experience." The main components it describes are:

- **Learning guides.** These replace textbooks. Their activities follow a prescribed sequence that starts with prior knowledge, introduces a new concept, and then applies it in known and in unknown or future situations.
- **Student government.** The study describes this as learning by doing.
- **Assessment.** The model stresses formative rather than summative assessment, with the accent on oral and written feedback rather than on repetitive testing.
- **Movement and play.** Every learning activity typically begins with a physical warm-up.
- **Community involvement.** VNEN schools encourage parents and other community members to take part in school activity.

The study also cautions that seating children in small groups while the teacher continues to direct the whole class from the front is not active learning.

### Training content

The study calls teacher training a crucial input of the programme. All primary school teachers in Vietnam receive two weeks of summer training, which was the initial VNEN training for most teachers. According to the study, the training event used an emulation of the VNEN classroom with active discussion rather than a lecture. Teachers also received materials before the event. The study describes the training as conducted in the form of a VNEN classroom, with groups, group leaders and warm-up games.

Sustained professional development was meant to continue through biweekly cluster meetings. The study notes that these were supposed to be held once every two weeks. It reports that teachers in VNEN schools attended an average of three to four per year, and that biweekly cluster training was not fully implemented in some schools.

### Implementation observations

The study reports that most VNEN schools had 75% or more of the classroom artifacts it lists. It also reports that 13% of VNEN teachers in 2014 and 11% in 2015 said they did not use the artifacts at all. It states that implementation was strongest where school leaders and teachers showed initiative and creativity in participative and collaborative learning.
