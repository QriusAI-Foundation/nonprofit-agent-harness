## Programme approach and training content

The programme's training material is ALNAP's *The State of the Humanitarian System* (SOHS), 2022 edition. This section describes what that material contains. It does not report on how training was delivered, who took part or what resulted, because the source document does not cover these.

### Nature and scope of the material

The SOHS describes itself as an independent longitudinal study of the international humanitarian system. The 2022 edition covers January 2018 to December 2021. Its content is organised around three framing questions, beginning with "What is the system?" It has 12 chapters, and each one presents evidence to answer a key question about the state of the system. A quick user guide signposts readers to key topics.

### Evidence base

The report draws its findings from 10 research components that combine primary data collection with synthesis of secondary data. These include:

- a survey of 5,487 aid recipients in six crisis contexts;
- a global web-based survey of practitioners and host-government representatives, with 436 completed responses;
- an evaluation synthesis, for which over 500 humanitarian evaluations were assessed for inclusion.

For this edition, the authors state that they consulted aid recipients on the design of the research itself for the first time. As a result, the report gives greater emphasis to recipients' priority issues: targeting, corruption, do no harm and accountability to affected populations.

### Analytical framework

The material sets out performance criteria adapted from the OECD-DAC criteria. Examples include:

- **Coverage:** the degree to which action reaches all people in need.
- **Effectiveness:** the degree to which operations meet their stated objectives.

The conclusion summarises how the system's performance against these criteria has changed since earlier editions.

### Stated limitations

The report acknowledges that persistent and pervasive data gaps limit its ability to give clear assessments on key performance issues. Any training content drawn from it carries the same caveat.
