## Programme approach and training content

**Source material.** The training material for the programme is the 2022 edition of The State of the Humanitarian System, produced by ALNAP. ALNAP describes itself as a global network of humanitarian organisations dedicated to learning how to improve the response to humanitarian crises.

**Structure of the content.** The report presents its findings through 12 chapters, each of which sets out evidence to answer a key question about the state of the system. Participants can navigate by topic: the authors provide a quick user guide that highlights key topics. One chapter addresses humanitarian principles, which the report describes as humanity, impartiality, neutrality and independence. The conclusion gives a longitudinal overview of system performance against the DAC evaluation criteria.

**Evidence base.** According to the report, its findings are drawn from 10 research components that combine primary data collection with secondary data synthesis. These include a survey of 5,487 aid recipients in six crisis contexts and a global web-based practitioner and host-government survey with 436 completed responses. They also include a synthesis of humanitarian evaluations: more than 500 were assessed for inclusion and over 130 were chosen for more in-depth analysis. The report also includes case studies drawing on original country research conducted by local researchers.

**Participatory design.** The report states that, for the first time, the research team consulted aid recipients on the design of the research itself. It reports that their views led the team to adjust its research questions and data collection plans. Targeting, corruption, do no harm and accountability to affected populations received greater emphasis as a result.

**Limitations to convey to participants.** The report is explicit about the limits of the evidence. It states that persistent and pervasive data gaps limit its ability to give clear, definitive assessments on key performance issues. Facilitators should present its findings with this caveat. The report does not claim that the training itself produces any particular outcome, and this section makes no such claim.
