## Programme approach and training content

The programme's training content is drawn from the World Health Organization's *Global curriculum guide for community health workers*, published in 2025. The guide adopts the principles and structure of competency-based education (CBE). It describes this as an approach with less emphasis on time-based training and more attention to mastery of knowledge, skills and competencies for practice. According to WHO, the guide is designed primarily for formal community health workers (CHWs) who are integrated into the health system, have a structured education pathway and a contract, and are remunerated and supported.

### Curriculum structure

The guide has three parts: competencies, organized into six domains; 12 universal modules; and four role-specific modules comprising 28 topics.

- **Competencies.** The guide identifies 25 competencies relevant to all CHWs across all contexts.
- **Universal modules.** These are described as relevant to all CHWs, with contextualization. Eleven are oriented to practice activities and one covers basic science knowledge. Titles include conducting a home visit, facilitating referrals and access to health services, and assessing community health needs. The guide suggests a total of 303 hours for the universal modules, including 60 hours for the basic science module.
- **Role-specific modules.** These cover areas including maternal and newborn health, HIV and sexually transmitted infections, tuberculosis, malaria, noncommunicable diseases, neglected tropical diseases, and health emergency preparedness and response. The guide states that no CHW can carry out all of these practice activities and tasks. It advises that content be selected based on population needs and CHWs' scope of practice in a given context.

### Learning and assessment methods

The guide proposes individual, small-group and shadowing learning experiences. It recommends that teaching minimize didactic approaches in favour of practical and authentic learning methods. Formative and summative assessments are outlined for each module. For example, the home visit module suggests 33 teaching hours, including a minimum of 5 hours of shadowing and 6 hours of summative assessment.

### Adaptation and limitations

WHO states that the guide is not intended to be interpreted as rigid or prescriptive. It does not contain the learning materials or case studies themselves. Tutors are encouraged to develop materials that are attractive, accessible and usable in a hands-on way. The guide also emphasizes that CHWs' acquisition and maintenance of knowledge, skills and attitudes depend not only on end-of-training assessment. They also depend on access to continuous training, supportive supervision and follow-up engagement.
