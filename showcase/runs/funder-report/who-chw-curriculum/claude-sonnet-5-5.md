## Programme approach and training content

The training material for this programme is the Global curriculum guide for community health workers, published by the World Health Organization in 2025. The guide states that its objective is to inform the design and implementation of CHW education and training curricula, with the aim of strengthening primary health care and essential services at community level. It is designed primarily for formal CHWs who are integrated into the health system, have a structured education pathway and a contract, and are appropriately remunerated and supported.

### Evidence base

According to the guide, its design drew on a scoping review of peer-reviewed literature that included 236 articles. It also drew on consultation with CHWs, including online focus groups with over 20 CHWs in Kenya, Sierra Leone, Togo and the United Republic of Tanzania.

### Structure and content

The guide has three parts: competencies, universal modules and role-specific modules. In total, the guide reports six competency domains, 12 universal modules and four role-specific modules covering 28 topics. It sets out 25 competencies that it describes as relevant to all CHWs across all contexts. The universal modules carry a suggested total duration of 303 hours. Module 12, on basic science, is intended to be completed before training on any role-specific content. Module 1, on conducting a home visit, is given as an example of the module format and has a suggested time of 33 teaching hours, including a minimum of 5 hours of shadowing and 6 hours for the summative assessment.

### Learning and assessment approach

The guide describes a competency-based approach in which the tutor acts as a facilitator rather than as someone who imparts knowledge. It outlines both formative and summative assessments for each module. It suggests practical learning methods including shadowing, role play, peer-to-peer learning, case-based learning, simulation, technical skills practice and supervised community-based learning. The guide says that it proposes learning activities but does not itself contain learning materials or case studies.

### Adaptation to context

The guide states that it is designed to be adapted to different health systems, programmes and contexts. For the role-specific modules, it advises selecting content according to the needs of the population and the scope of practice of CHWs in a given context. Any adaptation of this programme's training should follow that guidance.

This section describes the content of the source material only. The guide does not report results from this programme's own delivery.
