## Programme approach and training content

This section describes the approach and content of the training material, the World Health Organization's *Global curriculum guide for community health workers* (2025). Everything below is taken from that guide and attributed to it.

### Approach

The guide is competency-based and is intended to be adapted rather than applied as written. It states that it is designed for formal community health workers (CHWs) who are integrated into the health system, have a structured education pathway and a contract, and are appropriately remunerated and supported. It also states that the guide is meant to be adapted to different health systems, programmes and contexts.

For learning methods, the guide recommends that teaching minimize didactic approaches in favour of practical and authentic methods. It outlines both formative and summative assessments for each module. It also states that competence depends on more than end-of-training assessment, and relies as well on continuous training, supportive supervision and follow-up engagement.

### Structure and content

The guide has three parts: competencies, universal modules and role-specific modules. Overall, its content is organized into six competency domains, 12 universal modules and four role-specific modules containing 28 topics.

The universal modules are those relevant to all CHWs. Table 2.1 of the guide gives a total suggested duration of 303 hours for the universal practice activities.

The role-specific modules are a menu rather than a required package. The guide advises that their content be selected according to the needs of the population and the CHWs' scope of practice in a given context.

### Illustrative module

Module 1, on conducting a home visit, shows how the modules are specified. It suggests 33 teaching hours, including a minimum of 5 hours of shadowing and 6 hours for the summative assessment.

### Limits of the source

The guide describes itself as a reference and says it does not contain learning materials or case studies. Delivery materials therefore have to be developed separately. This section does not report numbers of CHWs trained or any outcomes, because the source document gives none.
