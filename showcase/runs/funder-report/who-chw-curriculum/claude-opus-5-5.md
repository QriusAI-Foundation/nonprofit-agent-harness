## Programme approach and training content

The training content described in this section is drawn from the *Global curriculum guide for community health workers*, published by the World Health Organization (WHO) in 2025. The description below reflects what the guide sets out. It does not report on delivery or results.

### Educational approach

The guide adopts the principles and structure of competency-based education (CBE). It describes CBE as oriented to outcome abilities, with less emphasis on time-based training. Under this approach, the guide states that the tutor's role becomes that of a facilitator rather than someone who imparts knowledge. Suggested learning activities include individual, small-group and shadowing experiences.

### Curriculum structure

The guide is organised into three parts:

- **Competencies:** six competency domains, comprising 25 competencies relevant to all community health workers (CHWs).
- **Universal modules:** 12 modules, made up of 11 practice-activity modules and one basic science module. The guide states that these form the basis of any curriculum developed or adapted from it.
- **Role-specific modules:** four modules covering 28 topics.

The tentative recommended duration for the universal modules totals 303 hours. The basic science module alone has a suggested time of 60 hours, including assessments.

The role-specific areas include:

- maternal and newborn health
- HIV and sexually transmitted infections
- tuberculosis
- malaria
- noncommunicable diseases
- neglected tropical diseases
- health emergency preparedness and response

The guide advises that role-specific content be selected according to population needs and the CHWs' scope of practice in a given context.

### Module design and assessment

Each practice-activity module describes:

- practice activities and tasks
- priority competencies
- learning objectives
- learning activities and assessments, with suggested durations

Formative and summative assessments are outlined for each module. Summative assessments are linked to progression decisions on readiness for practice.

As an example, Module 1 (conducting a home visit) has a suggested 33 teaching hours. This includes at least 5 hours of shadowing and 6 hours of summative assessment.

### Adaptation and limitations

The guide states that it is not intended to be rigid or prescriptive. It also notes that it does not contain the learning materials or case studies themselves; these would need to be developed separately.

The guide is designed primarily for formal CHWs who are integrated into the health system, have a structured education pathway and a contract, and are appropriately remunerated and supported. It notes that most volunteer CHWs, or those working only a few hours a week, cannot reasonably carry out all the universal practice activities. It also describes health system support systems as essential for competency-based education to reach its full potential.
