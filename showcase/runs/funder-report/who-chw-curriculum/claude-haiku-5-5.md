## Programme approach and training content

The training approach draws on the WHO *Global curriculum guide for community health workers* (2025). The guide states that it adopts the principles and structure of competency-based education (CBE). Among the five core components of CBE programmes it lists is programmatic assessment of the achievement of learning. In CBE, the role of the tutor shifts towards facilitation rather than imparting knowledge.

The guide is designed mainly for CHWs who are formally integrated into the health system after a structured education pathway. It has three parts. The first section sets out competencies that all health workers, including CHWs, should possess. It lists 25 competencies relevant to all CHWs across all contexts, organized into six domains.

The second part comprises universal modules. The guide describes 11 modules oriented to practice activities and one module on basic science knowledge. Its table of universal modules gives a total of 303 hours of universal practice activities, and Module 1, Conducting a home visit, is suggested at 33 teaching hours. Both formative and summative assessments are outlined for each module.

The third part comprises role-specific modules. The guide states that no CHW can carry out all of the practice activities and tasks in these modules. Module 13 covers eight health topics and Module 16 covers four population-based service areas. Tutors should select only relevant health topics to tailor the programme to the roles and responsibilities of the CHWs.

The guide's content was informed by a scoping review of 236 peer-reviewed articles and a grey literature search covering 16 countries. Drafts were shared with the WHO Technical Advisory Group, and CHWs from a range of settings also reviewed the draft. The guide describes itself as only a pointer or reference to what can be included in CHW trainings. It recommends updating curricula periodically, for example at least every 5–10 years.
