## Programme approach and training content

The programme's training material draws on the WHO Global curriculum guide for community health workers, published in 2025. The guide is described as competency-based. It is a reference for local adaptation, not a fixed programme: this competency-based CHW curriculum guide is designed to be adapted to the needs of different health systems, programmes and contexts, and it is not intended to be interpreted as rigid or prescriptive.

The guide has three parts. There are three parts to this curriculum guide. The first covers competencies, with 25 competencies relevant to all CHWs across all contexts, organized into six domains. The second covers universal modules: there are 11 modules oriented to practice activities, and one module relating to the common basic science knowledge required for practice. The total suggested duration of universal practice activities is 303 hours.

Universal modules are relevant to all CHWs, with contextualization. For example, module 1 can be used to prepare CHWs to conduct a home visit, and module 2 includes tasks such as distributing health commodities such as bednets, soaps and pedometers. Module 12 outlines basic biology, anatomy, physiology and pathology, and its suggested time is 60 hours, including assessments.

The third part covers role-specific modules, which address service areas that may or may not form part of a CHW's role. These areas include maternal and newborn health, HIV and STIs, TB, malaria, NCDs, NTDs, and health emergency preparedness and response. No CHW can carry out all of the practice activities and tasks outlined in the role-specific modules in this framework, so content should be selected to the scope of practice in a given context. Learners should be trained using all 12 sessions, but only with material that is relevant to the planned scope of practice of the CHW being trained.

Learning activities are suggested throughout the modules, oriented to the learning objectives and achievement of the module learning outcome. Both formative and summative assessments of learning are outlined for each module. Module 1 includes shadowing of a CHW through home visits, with a reflective debrief, and its suggested time is 33 teaching hours.

The guide does not supply finished learning materials. While it proposes learning activities that often draw from presentations, case scenarios or role play scenario cards, it does not contain the learning materials or case studies. Materials should be in a language and format relevant to the CHWs being trained.

The guide's evidence base includes a scoping review in which 236 peer-reviewed articles were extracted, together with a targeted grey literature search. CHWs from a range of geographical and income settings also reviewed the draft curriculum. For adaptation, curriculum developers should begin by selecting only those health topics relevant to their particular context.
