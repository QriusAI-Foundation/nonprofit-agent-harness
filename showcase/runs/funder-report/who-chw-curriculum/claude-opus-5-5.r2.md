## Programme approach and training content

The programme's training material is the *Global curriculum guide for community health workers*, published by the World Health Organization (WHO) in 2025. This section summarises what the guide sets out. It does not report on delivery, participation or outcomes.

### Educational approach

The guide adopts the principles and structure of competency-based education (CBE). In this model, the tutor acts as a facilitator rather than someone who imparts knowledge. The suggested learning activities combine individual work, small-group work and shadowing. The guide outlines both formative and summative assessments for each module.

The guide is designed primarily for formal community health workers (CHWs). These are CHWs who are integrated into the health system, have a structured education pathway and a contract, and are appropriately remunerated and supported.

### Structure of the content

The guide has three parts, organised into:

- **Six competency domains** containing 25 competencies, which the guide describes as relevant to all CHWs across all contexts.
- **Twelve universal modules**: 11 practice-activity modules and one basic science module. The guide states these are relevant to all CHWs, with contextualisation. Its indicative total for the universal practice activities is 303 hours. This includes 60 hours for the basic science module, assessments included.
- **Four role-specific modules** comprising 28 topics. The areas covered include:
  - maternal and newborn health
  - HIV and sexually transmitted infections
  - tuberculosis
  - malaria
  - noncommunicable diseases
  - neglected tropical diseases
  - health emergency preparedness and response

Each practice-activity module in both the universal and role-specific sets follows a common set of components. For example, the guide suggests 33 teaching hours for Module 1 (Conducting a home visit). This includes at least 5 hours of shadowing and a 6-hour summative assessment, of which 3 hours are practice-based observation.

### Adaptation

The guide states that it is not intended to be rigid or prescriptive. It notes that no CHW can carry out all of the practice activities in the role-specific modules. Role-specific content should therefore be selected according to population needs and the CHW's scope of practice in a given context.

The guide proposes learning activities but does not contain the learning materials or case studies themselves. These would need to be developed separately.
