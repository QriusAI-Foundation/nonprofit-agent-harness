## Programme approach and training content

The programme's training content draws on the World Bank's *World Development Report 2018: Learning to Realize Education's Promise* (WDR 2018). This section summarises what that material covers. It does not report programme delivery, participation or results.

### Framing of the material

The WDR 2018 describes itself as the first World Development Report devoted entirely to education. Its starting premise is that schooling is not the same as learning. It is organised around four main themes.

### Core policy framework

The material sets out three complementary strategies, which give the training its main structure:

- **Assess learning**, to make learning a serious goal.
- **Act on evidence**, to make schools work for all learners.
- **Align actors**, to make the whole education system work for learning.

### Measurement of learning

The material treats formative classroom assessment as a tool that supports instruction. It also offers practical tips for effective learning measurement, including:

- measuring gaps;
- tracking progress; and
- testing students when effective action is still possible.

### School-level practice

On learner preparation, the material recommends setting children on high-development trajectories through early childhood interventions.

On teaching, it covers three principles:

- teacher training should be designed to be individually targeted;
- teaching should be targeted to the level of the student; and
- pecuniary and nonpecuniary incentives can be used to improve teacher motivation.

On school inputs, including technology, the material advises providing them in ways that complement rather than substitute for teachers.

### System-level change

At the system level, the material presents action on three fronts:

- investing in better information on learning;
- mobilising coalitions for learning; and
- adopting a more iterative, adaptive approach to change.
