## Programme approach and training content

The programme's training material is drawn from the World Bank's 2018 World Development Report, *LEARNING to Realize Education's Promise*. The Report frames the programme's approach around three strategies: assessing learning to make it a serious goal, acting on evidence to make schools work for all learners, and aligning actors to make the whole system work for learning.

The training content begins from the premise that schooling is not the same as learning. The Report notes that in Kenya, Tanzania, and Uganda, three-quarters of grade 3 students in one assessment did not understand a simple sentence, and that children from poor households learn the least. Learning should therefore be treated as the central goal of education, not only enrolment.

The material on learner preparation highlights early childhood nutrition, stimulation, and care, and notes that stunting and deprivation affect brain development. It also addresses demand-side barriers, including school fees and opportunity costs, and recommends remedial education before further training.

The teacher-focused material states that teachers are the most important school-level factor affecting learning. It identifies three principles: teacher training should be individually targeted and repeated with follow-up coaching; teaching should be pitched to the level of the student; and incentives should target actions that are within teachers' capacity. The Report observes that most teacher training evaluated is ineffective, and that long-term coaching in Africa and South Asia produced sizeable gains.

The material on school inputs and management emphasises that inputs such as technology and textbooks improve learning only when they strengthen the teacher-learner interaction. It notes that technology often fails when it is not implementable in existing systems, and that school management training for principals can affect learning.

Finally, the system-level material addresses misalignment between learning goals, finance, and incentives, and the role of coalitions and iterative, adaptive reform. The Report's examples, including England, Chile, and Malaysia, are presented as illustrations of the approaches the Report describes rather than as outcomes of this programme.

## Evidence cited

1. The Report's framing is that schooling is not the same as learning.
   > "Schooling is not the same as learning." (OVERVIEW)
2. The Report states that in Kenya, Tanzania, and Uganda, three-quarters of grade 3 students did not understand a simple sentence in a recent assessment.
   > "In Kenya,  Tanzania, and Uganda, when grade 3 students were asked recently to read a sentence such as “The name of the dog is Puppy,” three-quarters did not understand what it said." (OVERVIEW)
3. The Report frames its approach around three strategies: assess learning, act on evidence, and align actors.
   > "Assess learning—to make it a serious goal." (OVERVIEW)
4. The Report states that within countries, learning outcomes are almost always much worse for the disadvantaged.
   > "Within countries, learning outcomes are almost always much worse for the disadvantaged." (OVERVIEW)
5. The Report states that poor children learn the least and that the learning gap widens over time.
   > "Poor children learn the least, which hurts them the most" (Chapter 3: The many faces of the learning crisis)
6. The Report states that disadvantaged children's learning gaps are evident before school begins.
   > "Children from disadvantaged backgrounds tend to exhibit learning deficits years before they start school" (What is causing the learning crisis?)
7. The Report states that severe deprivation impairs infants' brain development.
   > "Severe deprivations—whether in terms of nutrition, unhealthy environments, or lack of nurture by caregivers—have long-lasting effects because they impair infants’ brain development." (OVERVIEW)
8. The Report identifies early childhood nutrition, care, stimulation, and learning opportunities as a key entry point for learner preparation.
   > "Set children on high-development trajectories through early childhood nutrition, stimulation, and care." (Act on evidence—to make schools work for all learners)
9. The Report states that fees and opportunity costs remain major financial barriers to schooling.
   > "Fees and opportunity costs are still major financial barriers to schooling" (OVERVIEW)
10. The Report recommends that remediation be provided before further education and training.
   > "To make up for the fact that so many youth lack skills  when leaving basic education, provide remediation before further education and training." (Act on evidence—to make schools work for all learners)
11. The Report states that teachers are the most important factor affecting learning in schools.
   > "Teachers are the most important factor affecting learning in schools." (OVERVIEW)
12. The Report states that most teacher training is ineffective, though some approaches work.
   > "Most teacher training is ineffective, but some approaches work" (Chapter 6: Teacher skills and motivation both matter)
13. The Report recommends that teacher training be individually targeted and repeated, with follow-up coaching.
   > "For effective teacher training, design it to be individually targeted and repeated, with follow-up coaching" (Act on evidence—to make schools work for all learners)
14. The Report states that programs in Africa and South Asia that provided long-term coaching led to sizable learning gains.
   > "By contrast, programs in Africa and South Asia that provided long-term coaching led to sizable learning gains." (Make teaching more effective)
15. The Report recommends targeting teaching to the level of the student so learners do not fall behind.
   > "To keep learners from falling behind to the point where they cannot catch up, target teaching to the level of the student." (Make teaching more effective)
16. The Report recommends that incentives be used to improve teacher motivation, ensuring the incentivized actions are within teachers' capacity.
   > "Use pecuniary and nonpecuniary incentives to improve the motivation of teachers, ensuring that the incentivized actions are within teachers’ capacity." (Make teaching more effective)
17. The Report states that additional inputs should complement rather than substitute for teachers.
   > "Provide additional  inputs, including new technologies, in ways that complement rather than substitute for teachers." (Focus everything else on teaching and learning)
18. The Report states that technology interventions often fail because they are not implementable in current systems.
   > "Ensure that new information and communication technology is really implementable in the current systems." (Align actors—to make the whole system work for learning)
19. The Report states that training principals in how to improve teacher-learner interaction has led to a large impact on student learning.
   > "Training principals in how to improve that interaction—by providing feedback to teachers on lesson plans, action plans to improve student performance, and classroom behavior—has led to a large impact on student learning." (Align actors—to make the whole system work for learning)
20. The Report states that misalignment in education systems arises from technical and political barriers.
   > "Countries must also overcome technical and political barriers" (OVERVIEW)
21. The Report states that an iterative, problem-driven approach to reform can help systems adapt interventions to local contexts.
   > "Recent examples show how an iterative, adaptive approach can strengthen education systems and improve learning." (Encouraging innovation and agility)
22. The Report describes England's literacy and numeracy reforms as having improved primary schoolchildren's skills.
   > "Since 1995, England has substantially improved the literacy and numeracy skills of primary schoolchildren using good political strategy and sound technical solutions." (How to escape low-learning traps)
