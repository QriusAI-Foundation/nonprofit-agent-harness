# Board brief: WHO Global curriculum guide for community health workers (2025)

**What it is.** This is a World Health Organization guide. It helps people design training for community health workers (CHWs). It is a framework, not a ready-made course. Countries and programmes are told to adapt it to local needs.

## Key points

1. **Purpose.** The guide helps groups plan, design and run CHW training. The aim is better essential health services in communities.
2. **Who it is for.** It is written mainly for formal, paid CHWs with contracts. WHO says most volunteers, or people working only a few hours a week, cannot reasonably do all the core activities it describes.
3. **Time commitment.** The core ("universal") modules alone add up to 303 suggested hours of training.
4. **Pick only what fits.** The role-specific modules are a menu. WHO says no CHW can do all of these tasks. Programmes should choose only what matches their workers' real roles.
5. **No ready-made materials.** The guide does not include learning materials or case studies. Any organisation using it would need to create or find its own.
6. **Training must match support.** WHO advises against training CHWs in tasks they lack the time or support to carry out.

## What the board should decide or watch

- **Fit.** If our organisation relies on volunteers or part-time workers, the full curriculum may not suit us. We should decide which parts, if any, are relevant.
- **Scope.** Ask staff to name the specific roles our workers actually fill. Then select only the matching modules.
- **Resources.** Budget for the staff time to build training materials, since the guide does not supply them.
- **Support.** Before adding new training, check that workers will have the time and backing to use it.

## Evidence cited

1. The guide was developed to help design and implement CHW training to strengthen essential community services.
   > "This guide was developed to assist in the conceptualization, design and implementation of CHW curricula with a view to strengthening delivery of essential services at community level as an integral part of primary health care." (Executive summary)
2. Most volunteer or part-time CHWs cannot reasonably carry out all the universal practice activities.
   > "Most volunteer CHWs, or those working only a few hours a week, cannot reasonably carry out all the universal practice activities expected of all CHWs in this framework." (1.2 Objective and target audience)
3. The universal modules total 303 suggested hours.
   > "Total duration of universal practice activities (hours) 303" (Table 2.1 Universal modules: practice activities and suggested duration)
4. No CHW can perform all the role-specific tasks, so content must be selected.
   > "No CHW can carry out all of the practice activities and tasks outlined in the role-specific modules in this framework." (3.1 Overview of the CHW curriculum guide)
5. The guide does not include learning materials or case studies.
   > "it does not contain the learning materials or case studies." (Creating user-friendly learning materials appropriate to CHWs)
6. CHWs should not be trained in tasks they lack time or support to perform.
   > "In general, CHWs should not be trained in practice activities that they do not reasonably have the time or support to perform" (4. Health policy and health system support for CHW integration in primary health care)
