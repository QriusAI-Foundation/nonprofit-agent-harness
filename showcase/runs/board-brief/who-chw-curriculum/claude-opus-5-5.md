# Board brief: WHO Global curriculum guide for community health workers (2025)

## What this document is
This is a World Health Organization (WHO) guide for people who design training for community health workers (CHWs). It is not a ready-made course. It is a framework that organisations adapt to their own setting.

## Key points

1. **Purpose.** The guide helps organisations plan, design and run CHW training programmes.

2. **Volunteers are a special case.** The guide is written mainly for paid, formal CHWs. WHO says most volunteer CHWs, or CHWs who work only a few hours a week, cannot reasonably do all the core activities it describes.

3. **A core set for every CHW.** There are 11 core practice modules, such as home visits, referrals and community needs assessment. There is also one basic science module.

4. **Pick only what fits.** Extra role-specific topics, such as malaria, TB or diabetes, should be chosen based on local needs and on what CHWs actually do in that setting.

5. **Flexible, not fixed.** WHO says the guide should not be read as rigid or prescriptive.

6. **Do not over-train.** CHWs should not be trained in tasks they lack the time or support to carry out.

## What the board should decide or watch
- **Our CHW model.** Do we rely on volunteers or part-time workers? If so, we should not expect them to cover the full core set.
- **Topic choice.** Which role-specific topics match our community's needs and our CHWs' real duties?
- **Matching support to training.** Does every task we train for come with the time and support CHWs need to do it?

## Evidence cited

1. The guide helps organisations plan, design and run CHW training programmes.
   > "This guide was developed to assist in the conceptualization, design and implementation of CHW curricula" (Executive summary)
2. Most volunteer or part-time CHWs cannot reasonably do all the core activities the guide describes.
   > "Most volunteer CHWs, or those working only a few hours a week, cannot reasonably carry out all the universal practice activities expected of all CHWs in this framework." (1.2 Objective and target audience)
3. There are 11 core practice modules plus one basic science module.
   > "There are 11 modules oriented to practice activities, and one module relating to the common basic science knowledge required for practice." (3.1 Overview of the CHW curriculum guide)
4. Role-specific topics should be chosen based on local needs and on what CHWs do in that setting.
   > "Content from the role-specific modules should therefore be selected based on the needs of the population and the scope of practice of CHWs in a given context." (3.1 Overview of the CHW curriculum guide)
5. WHO says the guide should not be read as rigid or prescriptive.
   > "This guide is designed to facilitate design and implementation of CHW curricula, and is not intended to be interpreted as rigid or prescriptive." (3.3 How to use the curriculum guide)
6. CHWs should not be trained in tasks they lack the time or support to carry out.
   > "In general, CHWs should not be trained in practice activities that they do not reasonably have the time or support to perform" (4. Health policy and health system support for CHW integration in primary health care)
