# Board brief: WHO Global curriculum guide for community health workers (2025)

## What this is
The World Health Organization (WHO) published this guide in 2025. It helps people design training for community health workers (CHWs). It is a template, not a finished course.

## Key points
1. **Purpose.** The guide helps design and run CHW training. The aim is stronger essential services in communities, as part of primary health care.
2. **Who it is for.** Its main readers are CHW educators and people who write CHW curricula.
3. **Paid, supported workers.** It is built for formal CHWs with a contract and pay. WHO says most volunteer CHWs cannot do all the core activities.
4. **How it is built.** It has six competency domains. It has 12 universal modules and four role-specific modules. The role-specific modules hold 28 topics.
5. **Adapt it.** WHO says the guide should be adapted to each setting. The universal modules add up to 303 hours. Role-specific topics are picked by local need.
6. **Training needs support.** WHO says that full integration of CHWs has big resource costs. It points to national budgets as the source of support.

## What the board should decide or watch
- **Decide** whether any CHWs we train or fund are formal, paid and supported. If they are volunteers, the full guide may not fit.
- **Decide** which role-specific topics match our work. Do not try to teach all of them.
- **Watch** the costs of training time, supervision and pay.
- **Watch** for the need to review any curriculum we adopt as local needs change.

This brief covers only what the document says. It gives no advice beyond that.

## Evidence cited

1. The guide was developed to help conceive, design and implement CHW curricula, to strengthen essential services at community level within primary health care.
   > "This guide was developed to assist in the conceptualization, design and implementation of CHW curricula with a view to strengthening delivery of essential services at community level as an integral part of primary health care." (Executive summary)
2. The main target audience is CHW educators and those developing CHW curricula.
   > "The main target audience for this document comprises CHW educators as well those developing CHW curricula." (Executive summary)
3. The guide is designed for formal CHWs; most volunteer CHWs cannot reasonably carry out all the universal practice activities.
   > "Most volunteer CHWs, or those working only a few hours a week, cannot reasonably carry out all the universal practice activities expected of all CHWs in this framework." (1.2 Objective and target audience)
4. The content has six competency domains, 12 universal modules and four role-specific modules with 28 topics.
   > "The content is organized into six competency domains, 12 universal modules (11 practice activities plus one module of basic science knowledge), and four role-specific modules, comprising 28 role-specific topics organized under the four modules." (3.1 Overview of the CHW curriculum guide)
5. The guide should be adapted to different countries, health systems, programmes and contexts; the universal modules total 303 hours.
   > "This CHW curriculum guide should be adapted to the needs of different countries, health systems, programmes and contexts." (Executive summary)
6. Health system integration of CHWs has considerable resource implications, to be supported through national budgets.
   > "This level of health system integration has considerable resource implications, which should be supported also through inclusion of capital investments and regular recurrent costs in national budgets." (4. Health policy and health system support for CHW integration in primary health care)
