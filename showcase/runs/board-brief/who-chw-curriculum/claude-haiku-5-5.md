# Board brief: Global curriculum guide for community health workers

This brief covers a World Health Organization (WHO) guide published in 2025. It is a reference for planning training for community health workers (CHWs).

## 1. What the document is
A WHO guide on training CHWs. It is a global reference, not a finished course.

## 2. What it is for
It helps groups design, set up and run CHW training. Its aim is to strengthen essential community services as part of primary health care.

## 3. How it is built
It has three sections. Competencies come first. Universal modules come second and cover what all CHWs should learn. Role-specific modules come third, and users pick only those that fit their work.

## 4. Who it is for
The guide is aimed mainly at formal, paid CHWs with a contract. Most volunteers cannot carry out all the universal activities. Our board should ask whether this fits our volunteers before we rely on it.

## 5. How to use it
The guide is flexible, not a fixed script. Each place should adapt it to local needs, services and roles.

## 6. What to watch
The guide suggests reviewing curriculum content regularly, within a 5 to 10 year period. We should ask who owns that review in our organisation and when it is due.

## Board decisions
- Decide whether our volunteers fall within the target group.
- Ask staff to confirm how the guide would be used, and by whom.
- Note the review timing if we adopt any part of it.

## Evidence cited

1. The document is a WHO guide titled Global curriculum guide for community health workers, published in 2025.
   > "Global curriculum guide for community health workers" (Title)
2. The guide was developed to help design, implement and strengthen CHW curricula as part of primary health care.
   > "This guide was developed to assist in the conceptualization, design and implementation of CHW curricula with a view to strengthening delivery of essential services at community level as an integral part of primary health care." (Executive summary)
3. The second section of the guide holds universal modules, which cover material all CHWs need to know and practise.
   > "The second section of the guide comprises modules with materials that all CHWs need to know and be able to practise, hence labelled “universal modules”." (Executive summary)
4. The guide is designed mainly for formal CHWs with a contract, and most volunteer CHWs cannot carry out all the universal practice activities.
   > "Most volunteer CHWs, or those working only a few hours a week, cannot reasonably carry out all the universal practice activities expected of all CHWs in this framework." (1.2 Objective and target audience)
5. The guide is meant to be adapted and is not rigid or prescriptive.
   > "This guide is designed to facilitate design and implementation of CHW curricula, and is not intended to be interpreted as rigid or prescriptive." (3.3 How to use the curriculum guide)
6. The guide suggests that curriculum content be reviewed and revised at regular intervals, at least within a 5 to 10 year time frame.
   > "Consequently, it is suggested that countries and health systems, and especially officials with responsibility for CHW education policy, engage in a process of reviewing and revising curriculum content at regular intervals or at least within a 5–10-year time frame." (3.3 How to use the curriculum guide)
