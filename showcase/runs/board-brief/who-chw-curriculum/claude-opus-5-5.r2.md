# Board brief: WHO *Global curriculum guide for community health workers* (2025)

**What it is.** A World Health Organization guide for people who design and teach training for community health workers (CHWs). It is a framework, not a ready-made course. It helps shape what CHWs learn, how they learn it and how they are tested.

## Key points

1. **Purpose.** The guide helps design and run CHW training. The aim is better basic health services in communities.
2. **Built for paid, formal CHWs.** It mainly targets CHWs with contracts, pay and support. It says most volunteer or part-time CHWs cannot reasonably do all the core activities it expects.
3. **Three parts.** The guide has six areas of core skills. It has 12 modules that every CHW should learn. It also has four role-specific modules with 28 topics.
4. **Large time commitment.** The 12 universal modules alone add up to 303 suggested hours of training.
5. **Pick only what fits.** Role-specific content should be chosen to match local health needs and what CHWs actually do in that setting.
6. **Train only for what workers can do.** In general, CHWs should not be trained in tasks they do not have the time or support to perform.

## What this asks of an organisation

Any group that trains CHWs should adapt the guide to its own setting. It should not copy the guide whole. Its training should match the real role, time and support of its workers.

## What the board should decide or watch

- **Fit.** Do our CHWs match the paid, formal model the guide assumes? If they are volunteers, we should not expect them to cover the full core curriculum.
- **Scope.** Which role-specific topics match our actual services? We should cover those and leave the rest.
- **Capacity.** Can we fund and staff the training hours, supervision and support the guide links to good performance?
- **Alignment.** Training plans should line up with what our workers are asked, and supported, to do.

## Evidence cited

1. The guide was developed to help design and run CHW curricula to strengthen community-level essential services.
   > "This guide was developed to assist in the conceptualization, design and implementation of CHW curricula with a view to strengthening delivery of essential services at community level as an integral part of primary health care." (Executive summary)
2. Most volunteer or part-time CHWs cannot reasonably carry out all the universal practice activities in the framework.
   > "Most volunteer CHWs, or those working only a few hours a week, cannot reasonably carry out all the universal practice activities expected of all CHWs in this framework." (1.2 Objective and target audience)
3. The guide is organized into six competency domains, 12 universal modules and four role-specific modules with 28 topics.
   > "The content is organized into six competency domains, 12 universal modules (11 practice activities plus one module of basic science knowledge), and four role-specific modules, comprising 28 role-specific topics organized under the four modules." (3.1 Overview of the CHW curriculum guide)
4. The universal modules total 303 suggested hours.
   > "Total duration of universal practice activities (hours) 303" (Table 2.1 Universal modules: practice activities and suggested duration)
5. Role-specific content should be selected based on population needs and the CHWs' scope of practice.
   > "Content from the role-specific modules should therefore be selected based on the needs of the population and the scope of practice of CHWs in a given context." (3.1 Overview of the CHW curriculum guide)
6. CHWs generally should not be trained in activities they lack the time or support to perform.
   > "In general, CHWs should not be trained in practice activities that they do not reasonably have the time or support to perform" (4. Health policy and health system support for CHW integration in primary health care)
