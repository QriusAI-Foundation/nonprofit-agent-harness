# Board brief: Pathfinder CHBC Trainer's Guide (2006)

**What it is.** A training curriculum from Pathfinder International, dated October 2006. It teaches community health workers (CHWs) to give home-based care to people living with HIV/AIDS, to train family caregivers, and to help prevent HIV in their communities. It comes with a separate Handbook for participants. It was pre-tested in Tanzania and Mozambique and peer reviewed.

**What it asks of us.** Nothing directly. It is a free resource. We may copy or adapt it if we credit Pathfinder, do not sell it above cost, and send Pathfinder a copy of any adaptation.

**Key points**

1. **Purpose.** The guide aims to give CHWs the knowledge and skills to care for people with HIV/AIDS in their own communities.
2. **Licence.** We can reproduce or adapt any part without permission, as long as Pathfinder is acknowledged and the material is free or at cost.
3. **Size.** 13 core units, a practicum, and 5 advanced units (including family planning, PMTCT, ART support, and special groups). The sample agenda runs 24 days. This is a big time commitment for volunteers.
4. **Class size.** Training should be limited to 15-20 people so everyone gets skills practice.
5. **Trainers.** The guide suggests trainers be facility-based health providers who also supervise the CHWs afterwards. We would need a partnership with a clinic or hospital.
6. **Incentives.** CHWs are volunteers. The guide lists costs we should expect: travel stipends, bicycles, uniforms or badges, kit supplies, and income-generation training.

**What the board should decide or watch**

- Decide whether to adopt this curriculum, in full or in part, for our programme.
- Budget for CHBC kits, supplies, and incentives before training starts.
- Secure a clinical partner to train and supervise CHWs.
- Note the guide is from 2006. It tells trainers to check current local guidance, especially on medicines. We should do the same.

## Evidence cited

1. The guide aims to give CHWs the knowledge and skills to care for people with HIV/AIDS in their own communities.
   > "This curriculum aims to provide CHWs with the knowledge and skills necessary to provide CHBC to PLWHA in their communities." (Notes to the Trainer — Introduction to the Curriculum)
2. We can reproduce or adapt any part without permission, as long as Pathfinder is acknowledged and the material is free or at cost.
   > "Any part of this document may be reproduced or adapted to meet local needs without prior permission from Pathfinder International provided that Pathfinder International is acknowledged, and the material is made available free of charge or at cost." (Copyright page)
3. The guide contains 13 core units, a practicum, and 5 advanced units.
   > "The Trainer’s Guide is divided into 13 core units, a practicum, and 5 advanced units that contain additional information that CHWs might find useful after they have covered the core areas." (Notes to the Trainer — Introduction to the Curriculum)
4. Training should be limited to 15-20 people so everyone gets skills practice.
   > "CHBC trainings should be limited to 15-20 participants to ensure a good trainer-participant ratio and adequate time for skills practice." (Notes to the Trainer — Training and Supervision of Community Health Workers)
5. The guide suggests trainers also supervise the CHWs afterwards, linking community care to facility services.
   > "It is suggested that trainers double as supervisors of CHWs as they are in the best position to reinforce transferred skills and strengthen ties between CHBC and facility-based services." (Notes to the Trainer — Training and Supervision of Community Health Workers)
6. The guide lists incentive costs to expect: travel stipends, bicycles, uniforms or badges, kit supplies, and income-generation training.
   > "Examples of different incentives include travel stipends to reach clients’ homes, IGA training to support CHWs’ volunteerism, bicycles to reach far distances, uniforms or badges for identification and community recognition, replenishment of CHBC kit supplies as needed, and recognition of CHWs for their important work." (Notes to the Trainer — Incentives for CHW)
