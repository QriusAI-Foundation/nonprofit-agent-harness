# Board Brief: CHBC Trainer's Guide (Pathfinder International, October 2006)

**What it is.** This is a training guide. Community Home-Based Care (CHBC) is care for people living with HIV/AIDS, given in their homes and communities. The guide shows trainers how to teach community health workers (CHWs) to give that care.

**What it asks of an organisation.** It does not ask for money or a formal commitment. It sets out how to run the training well. The board should treat it as a plan to follow if the organisation runs or supports this training.

## Key points

1. **Purpose.** The guide aims to give CHWs the knowledge and skills to provide CHBC in their communities.

2. **Layout.** It has 13 core units, a practicum, and 5 advanced units. The advanced units cover extra topics for after the core areas.

3. **Flexible.** Units can be combined into a shorter course or a training series. Time, funding and local needs decide the shape. The board may need to decide how much to cover.

4. **Small groups.** Each training should have 15-20 participants. That keeps enough trainer time and practice time for everyone.

5. **Client consent.** Practice with real clients needs their informed consent. Clients can refuse, and their usual care must not change. The board should watch that this is followed.

6. **Ongoing support.** The guide says supervision and support must continue after training. The board should ask whether staff time and money are set aside for it.

## What the board should watch

- Whether consent is gained before any home practice.
- Whether training groups stay small.
- Whether supervision continues after the course ends.
- Whether the course is shaped to fit funding and local needs.

## Evidence cited

1. The guide aims to give CHWs the knowledge and skills to provide CHBC to people living with HIV/AIDS in their communities.
   > "This curriculum aims to provide CHWs with the knowledge and skills necessary to provide CHBC to PLWHA in their communities." (Notes to the Trainer: Introduction to the Curriculum)
2. The Trainer's Guide has 13 core units, a practicum, and 5 advanced units with additional information.
   > "The Trainer’s Guide is divided into 13 core units, a practicum, and 5 advanced units that contain additional information that CHWs might find useful after they have covered the core areas." (Notes to the Trainer: Introduction to the Curriculum)
3. The curriculum is flexible: units can be combined into a shorter course or training series depending on time, funding and local issues.
   > "This curriculum is also flexible, and different units can be put together for a shorter course, or a training series, depending on the time and funding available, and on the key issues being addressed in a particular setting." (Notes to the Trainer: Using this Curriculum)
4. Trainings should be limited to 15-20 participants to keep a good trainer-participant ratio and enough time for skills practice.
   > "CHBC trainings should be limited to 15-20 participants to ensure a good trainer-participant ratio and adequate time for skills practice." (Notes to the Trainer: Training and Supervision of Community Health Workers)
5. Clients must understand they can refuse to take part in the practicum without any effect on the care they already receive.
   > "Clients should understand that they can refuse to participate in the practicum and that it will not affect the care that they are already receiving." (Notes to the Trainer: More About the CHBC Practicum, Evaluation, and Certification)
6. Ongoing supervision and support is needed to help CHWs overcome challenges, ensure good quality services, and support learning between trainings.
   > "Ongoing supervision and support is needed to help the CHW overcome challenges, to ensure that PLWHA are getting good quality services, and to facilitate learning between trainings." (Notes to the Trainer: Training and Supervision of Community Health Workers)
