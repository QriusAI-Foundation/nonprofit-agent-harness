# Board brief: Vietnam Escuela Nueva (VNEN) impact evaluation

**What it is.** A 2017 World Bank study of a school reform in Vietnam. It follows students from Grade 3 to Grade 5. It compares VNEN schools with similar schools that were not in the program.

**What it asks of us.** Nothing directly. It is a research report on a national program. The board can use it as a case study of how to judge a program.

## Six key points

1. **Positive result.** The study says the program helped children's test scores and their social and emotional skills.

2. **Cost.** The program cost about US$85 million and reached about half a million children over four years.

3. **Doing it fully matters.** Results depend on how fully the program was carried out, not on the program name. Schools varied widely.

4. **Parents were not much involved.** Many parents never took part in classroom or school activities, even though the model expects them to.

5. **Training fell short.** Regular teacher meetings were held only now and then in many schools.

6. **Gains narrowed.** VNEN students' early lead shrank over the last two years of the study.

## What the board should watch

- When we judge our own programs, ask how fully they are carried out. A program's label is not enough.
- Check that staff training and community involvement actually happen as planned.
- Do not assume early gains will last.
- The authors say the statistical methods still need review in academic journals. Treat the headline findings with some care.

## Evidence cited

1. The study reports that VNEN had a positive impact on children's cognitive (test score) and non-cognitive (social and emotional) achievement.
   > "The VNEN program has had a positive impact on cognitive and non-cognitive achievement of children in Vietnam." (8.1 Positive Impact of VNEN)
2. The program cost about US$85 million and reached about half a million children over four years.
   > "The program cost approximately US$85 million, and benefited about a half million children over four years, not counting the children to be benefited in the future." (8.1 Positive Impact of VNEN)
3. Positive results depend on full implementation of VNEN elements, not on the program label.
   > "What drives the positive results of the program is not so much the label of VNEN or participation in the program as much as the full implementation of VNEN elements." (1.4 Preview of Findings)
4. Most VNEN parents reported never taking part in classroom activities, and over half never took part in school activities.
   > "However, a high 73% of VNEN parents report never having taken part in classroom activities and 54% reported never having taken part in school activities." (Takeaways: Parents Home and Community)
5. Biweekly teacher interaction (cluster training) happened only sporadically in many schools.
   > "Biweekly interaction between teachers appears to have taken place only sporadically in many schools." (8.4 Training of Teachers)
6. VNEN students' score advantage was kept over the years but became smaller over the last two years.
   > "This difference is retained throughout the years, though it narrows down over the last two years." (Executive Summary: Key Findings)
