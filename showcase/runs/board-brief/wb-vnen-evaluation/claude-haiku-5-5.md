Board brief: Vietnam Escuela Nueva Impact Evaluation Study (World Bank, 2017)

What this document is
This is a World Bank report on a school reform program in Vietnam called Vietnam Escuela Nueva (VNEN). It studies whether the program helped students learn. It covers the years 2013 to 2016.

What the program is
VNEN asks teachers to move from lectures to group work. Students use self-paced learning guides instead of textbooks. Students also run a class government. Parents and the community are asked to take part. The program reached about 1,447 schools.

What the study found
The report says VNEN had a positive effect on both test scores and social skills. The effect was larger where schools used the model fully. The report also says the program cost about US$40 per student per year. That is small compared with about US$1,000 per student for primary education.

Caveats
The schools were not chosen by random lottery. The authors used statistical matching instead. Many control schools also adopted parts of the model. Parent engagement was weak. The social skills measure relies on parent reports. The authors say more research is needed.

What the board should decide or watch
1. Ask whether our own work could use similar ideas, such as group learning and parent involvement.
2. Watch how well the schools follow the model. Full use mattered most.
3. Treat the strong claims with care. The report itself says the methods need academic review.
4. Note the report's advice: keep parents informed, support teachers through the year, and build feedback loops.

## Evidence cited

1. The document is a World Bank impact evaluation of the Vietnam Escuela Nueva program.
   > "Vietnam Escuela Nueva Impact Evaluation Study" (Title page)
2. The study covers the program's pilot and roll-out and the school years 2013 to 2016.
   > "The GPE-VNEN project and the larger VNEN program sought the implementation of this “whole school” approach in 1,447 schools from 2012–2016." (Executive Summary)
3. The report says the program had a positive impact on cognitive and non-cognitive achievement of children.
   > "The VNEN program has had a positive impact on cognitive and non-cognitive achievement of children in Vietnam, which should be recognized and made known to all stakeholders." (Policy Implications)
4. The program cost is roughly US$40 per student per year, against about US$1,000 per student for primary education.
   > "This works out roughly to about US$40 per student per year." (Policy Implications)
5. Because the schools were not randomly selected, the study used propensity score matching.
   > "Because the VNEN project had not selected participating schools through randomization, the study used a propensity score matching exercise to determine a matched panel of 650 control and treatment schools." (Executive Summary)
6. The report finds that full implementation of the model, not just the label, drives the results.
   > "What drives the positive results of the program is not so much the label of VNEN or participation in the program as much as the full implementation of VNEN elements." (1.4 Preview of Findings)
