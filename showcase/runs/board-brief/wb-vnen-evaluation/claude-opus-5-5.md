# Board Brief: Vietnam Escuela Nueva (VNEN) Impact Evaluation

## What this document is
This is a World Bank evaluation of VNEN, a school reform in Vietnam. VNEN uses group learning, student-led learning guides and stronger links with parents. The study followed one group of pupils from Grade 3 to Grade 5 and compared them with pupils in similar schools.

The report does not ask anything directly of our organisation. It is useful as evidence if we fund, run or advise on school programmes that use group learning.

## Key points

1. **It worked overall.** The report finds that VNEN improved both test results and social and emotional skills.

2. **It was cheap per child.** The authors put the cost at about US$40 per pupil per year. They judge this good value.

3. **Results depended on how well schools carried it out.** Some schools used the model fully and some did not. The authors warn that a complex reform cannot be forced on schools by order. They favour sharing what works.

4. **Parents were hard to involve.** Parents liked the idea of taking part, but in practice few did.

5. **Teacher support fell short.** Teachers were meant to meet every two weeks to learn from each other. In many schools these meetings happened only now and then.

6. **Treat the numbers with some care.** The authors say their methods still need review by other experts.

## What the board should decide or watch
- If we back a similar programme, should we budget for ongoing teacher support, not just start-up training?
- How will we check how fully each school uses the model, not only whether it joined?
- What is our plan to get parents taking part, not just agreeing?
- Should we wait for expert review of the findings before citing the results in our own materials?

## Evidence cited

1. The report finds VNEN had a positive impact on cognitive and non-cognitive achievement.
   > "The VNEN program has had a positive impact on cognitive and non-cognitive achievement of children in Vietnam." (8.1 Positive Impact of VNEN)
2. The programme cost roughly US$40 per student per year.
   > "This works out roughly to about US$40 per student per year." (Executive Summary – Findings Highlights)
3. Implementation varied across schools and a complex reform cannot be imposed by mandate.
   > "A complex reform cannot be enforced uniformly by mandate." (1.4 Preview of Findings)
4. Actual parent participation was sporadic.
   > "However, actual participation of parents was sporadic" (Executive Summary – Key Findings)
5. Biweekly teacher cluster meetings happened only sporadically in many schools.
   > "Biweekly interaction between teachers appears to have taken place only sporadically in many schools." (8.4 Training of Teachers)
6. The authors say the findings still need academic review.
   > "These findings need to be examined through the scrutiny of academic publication" (1.4 Preview of Findings)
