# Board brief: CBHFA Volunteer Manual (IFRC)

**What it is.** This is a training manual from the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies. It is for volunteers in the Community-based health and first aid (CBHFA) programme. It covers first aid, health promotion, disease prevention and disaster preparedness.

## Key points

1. **How it works.** Volunteers learn skills in a classroom and then use them in their own community. The manual calls this "learning by doing".

2. **A long-term commitment.** Taking up CBHFA is not a one-off project. It is a commitment to long-term health work. The board should be sure it can sustain this before starting.

3. **Core training is required.** The first three modules are compulsory. They prepare volunteers to help the community choose its own health priorities. Other topics are chosen after a community assessment.

4. **Manage expectations.** Volunteers must tell the community clearly what the programme can and cannot do. The manual says this is essential to avoid unrealistic hopes.

5. **Qualified first aid trainers.** Life-saving skills must be taught by qualified trainers recognised by the National Society. The board should check that such trainers are in place.

6. **Reporting.** Volunteers should fill in a report form after each household visit or activity. This shows whether the work is making a difference.

## What the board should decide or watch

- **Decide** whether the organisation can commit to long-term health programming.
- **Confirm** that recognised, qualified first aid trainers will deliver the skills training.
- **Watch** that volunteers set realistic expectations with the community.
- **Watch** that reports are completed and reviewed, so the board can see results.

## Evidence cited

1. Volunteers learn in the classroom and then act in the community, which the manual calls learning by doing.
   > "This is the learning by doing method: learn in the classroom, do in the community." (Introduction)
2. A National Society implementing CBHFA is committing to long-term health programming.
   > "A National Red Cross or Red Crescent Society implementing CBHFA is making a commitment to long-term health programming." (Foreword)
3. The first three modules are compulsory and prepare volunteers to help the community decide its priorities.
   > "The first three modules of the CBHFA materials are obligatory, and will give you the background skills you need to help your community make decisions about its priority concerns." (How CBHFA in action works)
4. Explaining what CBHFA can and cannot do is essential to avoid unrealistic expectations.
   > "This is imperative to avoid creating unrealistic expectations." (Talk to community groups or organizations)
5. Life-saving skills must be taught by qualified first aid trainers recognised by the National Society.
   > "These skills must be trained by qualified first aid trainers recognized by the National Society." (Topic 3: Basic life support (Part 1))
6. A report form should be completed after each household visit or community activity.
   > "A report form should be completed after each household visit or whenever CBHFA activities are performed in the community." (Topic 8: Reporting on CBHFA activities in my community)
