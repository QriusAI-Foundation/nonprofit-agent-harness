# Board Brief: The State of the Humanitarian System 2022

## What this document is
This is the 2022 edition of The State of the Humanitarian System (SOHS), published by ALNAP, a global network of humanitarian organisations. It is the fifth edition. It covers January 2018 to December 2021. It is an independent study that looks at how well the international humanitarian system is working.

## What it asks of organisations
The report asks humanitarians to learn and improve. It asks them to measure the difference they make, to listen to affected people, and to share power with local actors.

## Key points

1. **Funding has grown but not kept pace.** Aid reached an estimated $31.3 billion in 2021. Requirements in appeals grew much faster, and funding met only about half of them in 2020.

2. **Few donors provide most of the money.** Almost half of international aid came from five donors. The US gave about a third in 2021.

3. **Reach is incomplete.** The system reached an estimated 46% of people in need under the plans where data was available. Only 36% of aid recipients surveyed thought aid went to those who needed it most.

4. **Local actors still get little direct funding.** Direct funding to local and national actors was only about 1.2% of international aid in 2021.

5. **Attacks on aid workers rose.** Attacks rose by 54% in the four years to 2020. Most victims were national staff.

6. **Evidence of impact is weak.** Only four of 29 countries had mortality data available consistently year on year. Board members should ask what evidence their own programmes rely on.

## What the board should decide or watch
- Ask how our funding depends on a small number of donors.
- Ask how we gather feedback from the people we serve, and whether we act on it.
- Check how we share funds and decisions with local partners.
- Watch the risk to staff and partners in insecure settings.
- Ask for clearer outcome data, not only numbers reached.
