# Board brief: The State of the Humanitarian System 2022

## What this is
This is a long research report from ALNAP, a network of aid groups. It looks at how the global aid system performed. It does not make direct demands of our organisation. It gives evidence we can use to judge our own work.

## Six key points
1. **Scope.** The report covers January 2018 to December 2021.
2. **Money.** Aid needs have grown faster than funding. The funding gap has grown.
3. **Reach.** Aid recipients doubt that help goes to the right people. Only 36% thought aid went to those who needed it most.
4. **Local groups.** Local and national groups got no real rise in funding. They mostly act as sub-contractors with limited influence.
5. **Evidence.** Data on results is thin. Mortality data was consistently available in only 4 of 29 sampled responses.
6. **Principles.** Pressure on principled aid has grown, as assertive states and a weakened multilateral system make it harder to work.

## What the board should consider
- **Decide:** Do we ask the people we serve whether our aid reaches those who need it most?
- **Decide:** Are we treating local partners as equals, or only as sub-contractors?
- **Decide:** Do we collect enough evidence of our results?
- **Watch:** Funding pressure, and any limits on our freedom to work by our principles.

The report is large. Staff could read the in-brief sections of chapters 3, 4, 9 and 11 and report back.

## Evidence cited

1. The report covers the period from January 2018 to December 2021.
   > "This fifth edition covers the period from January 2018 to December 2021 inclusive." (Introduction)
2. The humanitarian funding gap has grown over the study period.
   > "The humanitarian funding gap has grown since the start of the study period." (Chapter 3: Is there enough aid? – In brief)
3. Only 36% of aid recipients surveyed thought aid went to those who needed it most.
   > "Only 36% of aid recipients surveyed thought that aid went to those who needed it most." (Chapter 4: Does humanitarian support reach the right people?)
4. Local and national groups saw no real funding increase and mostly worked as sub-contractors with limited influence.
   > "But there was no real increase in funding for local actors, and for the most part L/NNGOs continued to operate as sub-contractors, with limited influence." (Chapter 9: Does the international system enable local action? – In brief)
5. Mortality data was consistently available in only 4 of 29 sampled responses.
   > "Out of a sample of 29 countries with humanitarian responses only 4 had mortality data available consistently year on year." (Chapter 6: Does humanitarian action work? – In brief)
6. Pressure on the space for principled humanitarian action has increased over the past 10 years.
   > "Assertive states and a weakened multilateral system have meant that pressure on the space for principled humanitarian action has increased over the past 10 years." (Chapter 11: Does the system uphold its principles? – In brief)
