# Board brief: The State of the Humanitarian System 2022 (ALNAP)

## What this document is
This is the fifth edition of a large study by ALNAP, a global network of aid groups. It looks at how well international humanitarian aid worked from January 2018 to December 2021. It draws on surveys of aid recipients and aid workers, interviews, evaluations and funding data.

## What it asks of organisations
The report does not set rules for us. It does point to steady demands on aid groups: listen to the people we serve, explain who gets help and why, share power and money with local partners, and keep staff safe.

## Six key points
1. **More money overall.** Aid funding reached a record level in 2021, almost double the level of ten years earlier.
2. **But the gap is growing.** In 2020, appeals were only a little more than half funded, a record low.
3. **Fairness is a worry.** Only about a third of aid recipients surveyed felt aid reached those who needed it most.
4. **Listening pays off.** People who feel consulted are more likely to say aid fits their needs.
5. **Local groups still lack money and power.** Local and national groups saw no real rise in funding and mostly worked as sub-contractors.
6. **Aid work got more dangerous.** Attacks on aid workers rose by more than half between 2017 and 2020.

## What the board should decide or watch
- **Funding:** Plan for tight and uncertain funding. Do not assume income will keep pace with need.
- **Listening:** Check that we consult the people we serve and act on what they tell us.
- **Selection:** Make sure our criteria for who receives help are clear and well explained.
- **Partners:** Review whether our local partners have a real say in decisions and a fair share of funds.
- **Staff safety:** Confirm that our safety plans cover all staff, including national staff.

## Evidence cited

1. Humanitarian funding reached an estimated $31.3 billion in 2021, almost double the level of a decade earlier.
   > "Financially it is larger than ever, reaching an estimated $31.3 billion in 2021 – almost double what it was a decade before." (Chapter 2: What is the shape and size of the humanitarian system? – In brief)
2. In 2020, appeals were little more than half funded, a record low.
   > "While the COVID-19 pandemic drove a $39.3 billion peak in appeals requirements in 2020, funding rose modestly, meaning the appeals were little more than half-funded – a record low." (Chapter 3: Is there enough aid? – In brief)
3. Only about a third of aid recipients surveyed felt aid reached those most in need.
   > "Only 36% of aid recipients surveyed thought that aid went to those who needed it most." (Chapter 4: Does humanitarian support reach the right people? – In brief)
4. People who feel consulted are more likely to say aid fits their needs.
   > "There is a clear relationship between how well people feel they are consulted and how well they think aid matches what they most need." (Chapter 5: Listening to affected people)
5. Local groups saw no real rise in funding and mostly worked as sub-contractors.
   > "But there was no real increase in funding for local actors, and for the most part L/NNGOs continued to operate as sub-contractors, with limited influence." (Chapter 9: Does the international system enable local action? – In brief)
6. Attacks on aid workers rose by more than half between 2017 and 2020.
   > "In the four years between 2017 and 2020, there was a 54% rise in the number of aid workers attacked" (Chapter 4: Attacks on aid workers)
