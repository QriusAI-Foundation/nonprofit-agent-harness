## Programme approach and training content

Our community health worker (CHW) training draws on *Community Home-Based Care for People and Communities Affected by HIV/AIDS: A Comprehensive Training Course for Community Health Workers*, a Trainer’s Guide published by Pathfinder International in 2006.

### Basis of the curriculum

The curriculum draws on Pathfinder’s experience of implementing community home-based care (CHBC) in Kenya, Tanzania, Ethiopia, Nigeria and Uganda. It is intended as a global model that can be adapted to local contexts. According to the guide, it was pre-tested in Tanzania and Mozambique and underwent internal and external peer review.

### Aims

The guide states that the course aims to give CHWs the knowledge and skills needed to provide CHBC to people living with HIV/AIDS (PLWHA) in their communities. Its stated goal is to add skilled CHWs to the forces fighting the HIV/AIDS epidemic. The guide specifies that the CHW’s main responsibility should be to transfer nursing and support skills to caregivers.

### Structure and content

The materials consist of a Trainer’s Guide for trainers and a Handbook for participants. The Trainer’s Guide is divided into 13 core units. These are followed by a practicum and five advanced units. The core training includes skills in basic nursing. It also covers prevention and management of opportunistic infections and guidance on when to refer clients to a health facility. One advanced unit provides an introduction to family planning. Another addresses preventing mother-to-child transmission of HIV.

### Training approach

The guide describes the training as participatory and based on adult learning principles. It calls for a focus on learning by doing. It recommends that trainings be limited to 15–20 participants. Where possible, at least one trainer should be a facility-based provider. The methods described include demonstrations and return demonstrations. The guide advises that lecture be used less than more participatory methods because it promotes passive learning. It notes that CHWs’ literacy levels can vary greatly. Trainers are asked to adapt the curriculum accordingly.

### Practicum and evaluation

The curriculum includes three practicums covering client care and community mobilisation. Informed consent is to be obtained from PLWHA well before the practicum. Pre- and post-tests are to be given to all participants. According to the guide, these tests serve to evaluate the effectiveness of the training.
