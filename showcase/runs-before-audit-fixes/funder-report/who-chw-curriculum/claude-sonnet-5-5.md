## Programme approach and training content

**Source and purpose.** The training material for this programme is the *Global curriculum guide for community health workers*, published by the World Health Organization in 2025. The guide states that it is designed to be adapted to different health systems, programmes and contexts. It is aimed primarily at formal community health workers (CHWs) who are integrated into the health system, follow a structured education pathway, hold a contract, and are appropriately remunerated and supported.

**Competency-based approach.** The guide adopts competency-based education (CBE). Per the guide, CBE prepares learners for practice oriented to outcome abilities. It places less emphasis on time-based training and more on mastery of knowledge, skills and competencies, together with accountability, flexibility and lifelong learning. The guide sets out 25 competencies that it describes as relevant to all CHWs across all contexts.

**Content structure.** The guide organizes its content into six competency domains, 12 universal modules (11 practice activities plus one basic science module), and four role-specific modules covering 28 topics. The universal modules, such as Module 1 on conducting a home visit, carry a suggested total duration of 303 hours. The guide describes the role-specific modules as a menu to select from, noting that no CHW can carry out all of the practice activities and tasks they contain.

**Learning methods and assessment.** The guide recommends that teaching minimize didactic approaches and use a diverse array of practical and authentic learning methods. It outlines both formative and summative assessments for each module. As an illustration, Module 1 suggests 33 teaching hours, including a minimum of 5 hours of shadowing and 6 hours for the summative assessment, of which 3 hours are practice-based observation.

**Evidence base for the guide.** The guide reports that its design was informed by a scoping review that extracted 236 peer-reviewed articles. All figures and recommendations in this section are the guide's own. This section does not report results from delivering the training.
