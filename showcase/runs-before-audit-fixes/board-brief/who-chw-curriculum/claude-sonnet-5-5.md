# Board brief: WHO Global curriculum guide for community health workers (2025)

## What it is
This is a guide from the World Health Organization. It helps people build training courses for community health workers (CHWs). It is not a law or a standard we must meet.

## Key points

1. **Purpose.** The guide helps design and run CHW training. The aim is stronger essential services in communities, as part of primary health care.

2. **Who it is for.** Its main readers are CHW educators and people who write CHW curricula.

3. **Volunteers.** The guide warns that most volunteer CHWs, or those who work only a few hours a week, cannot do all the universal activities it lists. This matters if our own workers are volunteers.

4. **How it is built.** The content has six competency domains, 12 universal modules and four role-specific modules. The role-specific modules hold 28 topics.

5. **Pick only what fits.** No one is expected to teach everything. Planners should choose only the modules that suit their own health system.

6. **Pay and support.** The guide says CHWs should receive a financial package that matches their job demands, hours, training and roles.

## What the board should decide or watch
- Decide whether we train workers, and if so, whether this guide is a useful reference.
- If we use it, choose only the role-specific topics that match what our workers actually do.
- Check whether our workers are paid or volunteer. The guide is aimed mainly at formal, supported CHWs.
- Watch the cost of training, supervision and pay before we commit to anything.

## Evidence cited

1. The guide was developed to help design and implement CHW curricula to strengthen essential community services within primary health care.
   > "This guide was developed to assist in the conceptualization, design and implementation of CHW curricula with a view to strengthening delivery of essential services at community level as an integral part of primary health care." (Executive summary)
2. The main audience is CHW educators and those developing CHW curricula.
   > "The main target audience for this document comprises CHW educators as well those developing CHW curricula." (Executive summary)
3. Most volunteer or very part-time CHWs cannot carry out all the universal practice activities in the framework.
   > "Most volunteer CHWs, or those working only a few hours a week, cannot reasonably carry out all the universal practice activities expected of all CHWs in this framework." (1.2 Objective and target audience)
4. The guide has six competency domains, 12 universal modules and four role-specific modules with 28 topics.
   > "The content is organized into six competency domains, 12 universal modules (11 practice activities plus one module of basic science knowledge), and four role-specific modules, comprising 28 role-specific topics organized under the four modules." (3.1 Overview of the CHW curriculum guide)
5. For role-specific activities, planners are advised to select only the modules relevant to their health system.
   > "For the role-specific activities, programme managers or CHW educators are advised to select only the relevant modules appropriate to their health system." (1. Introduction)
6. The guide recommends that practising CHWs receive a financial package matched to job demands, hours, training and roles.
   > "Providing practising CHWs with a financial package commensurate with the job demands, complexity, number of hours worked, training and roles that they undertake." (4. Health policy and health system support for CHW integration in primary health care)
