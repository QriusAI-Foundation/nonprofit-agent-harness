# Board brief: WHO global curriculum guide for community health workers (2025)

This is a World Health Organization guide. It helps educators design training for community health workers (CHWs). It is a reference, not a rigid rulebook. The board does not need to approve it, but it helps us check whether our CHW training fits a clear standard.

## Key points

1. **What it is.** A guide to planning CHW education, built around competencies. It sets out what CHWs should be able to do.
2. **Who it is for.** Mainly formal CHWs who have a training pathway, a contract and support. Volunteers who work only a few hours a week will find many tasks out of reach.
3. **How it is built.** Competencies first. Then 12 universal modules and four role-specific modules, with many topics to choose from.
4. **How to use it.** Tutors should adapt it to local needs and select only the topics that fit their role.
5. **How often to review it.** Curricula should be revised on a regular cycle, at least every 5 to 10 years.
6. **What makes it work.** Pay, supervision, supplies and funding all shape how well CHWs perform.

## What the board should decide or watch

- Decide whether our CHWs are formal, paid and supported. If they are not, the full guide may be more than we need.
- Ask staff which training we use and when it was last reviewed.
- Watch the support systems the guide says are needed. Gaps in pay, supervision or supplies will limit results.
- Treat the guide as advice, not a requirement. Check it against our own local context.

## Evidence cited

1. The guide exists to inform the design and delivery of CHW education and training curricula.
   > "The objective of this global competency-based curriculum guide for CHWs is to inform the conceptualization, design and implementation of CHW education and training curricula" (1.2 Objective and target audience)
2. The guide is designed mainly for formal CHWs who are integrated into the health system, and it notes that most volunteer CHWs cannot carry out all the expected activities.
   > "This curriculum guide is designed primarily for formal CHWs who are integrated into the health system" (1.2 Objective and target audience)
3. The guide has competencies, 12 universal modules and four role-specific modules.
   > "The content is organized into six competency domains, 12 universal modules (11 practice activities plus one module of basic science knowledge), and four role-specific modules, comprising 28 role-specific topics organized under the four modules." (3.1 Overview of the CHW curriculum guide)
4. The guide is meant to be adapted to local needs and is not intended to be rigid or prescriptive.
   > "This guide is designed to facilitate design and implementation of CHW curricula, and is not intended to be interpreted as rigid or prescriptive." (3.3 How to use the curriculum guide)
5. Curricula should be reviewed and revised at regular intervals, at least within a 5 to 10 year time frame.
   > "reviewing and revising curriculum content at regular intervals or at least within a 5–10-year time frame" (3.3 How to use the curriculum guide)
6. Policies on pay, retention, supervision, supply chain and funding underpin CHW performance.
   > "Policies for worker compensation, retention, motivation, support, supervision, supply chain and funding underpin CHW performance." (4. Health policy and health system support for CHW integration in primary health care)
