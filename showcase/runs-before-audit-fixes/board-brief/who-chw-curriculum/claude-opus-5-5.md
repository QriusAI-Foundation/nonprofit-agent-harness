# Board brief: WHO Global curriculum guide for community health workers (2025)

## What this is
This is a World Health Organization guide published in 2025. It helps groups design training for community health workers (CHWs). It is a framework, not a ready-made course.

## Key points

1. **Purpose.** The guide helps organisations plan, design and run CHW training. The aim is better essential health services in communities, as part of primary health care.

2. **It is built for paid, formal CHWs.** The guide says most volunteer CHWs, or those working only a few hours a week, cannot reasonably do all the core activities it expects. This matters if our workers are volunteers or part-time.

3. **Pick only what fits.** There is a core set of modules for all CHWs. There is also a large menu of topic modules, such as malaria or diabetes. Topic modules should be chosen based on local needs and what our CHWs actually do.

4. **The core training is long.** The core modules add up to 303 hours. That is before any topic modules.

5. **No ready-made materials.** The guide suggests activities but does not include the learning materials or case studies. We would need to write or find our own.

6. **Do not train for work people cannot do.** CHWs should not be trained in tasks they do not have the time or support to perform. Emergency short courses are the exception.

## What the board should decide or watch
- **Decide** whether this guide fits our workforce, given its focus on formal, paid CHWs.
- **Decide** which topic modules match our real services, if we use it.
- **Budget** for staff time to build training materials.
- **Watch** that training plans match the hours, supervision and support we can really offer.

## Evidence cited

1. The guide was developed to help design and implement CHW curricula to strengthen essential community services as part of primary health care.
   > "This guide was developed to assist in the conceptualization, design and implementation of CHW curricula with a view to strengthening delivery of essential services at community level as an integral part of primary health care." (Executive summary)
2. Most volunteer or part-time CHWs cannot reasonably carry out all the universal practice activities in the framework.
   > "Most volunteer CHWs, or those working only a few hours a week, cannot reasonably carry out all the universal practice activities expected of all CHWs in this framework." (1.2 Objective and target audience)
3. Role-specific content should be selected based on population needs and the CHWs' scope of practice.
   > "Content from the role-specific modules should therefore be selected based on the needs of the population and the scope of practice of CHWs in a given context." (3.1 Overview of the CHW curriculum guide)
4. The universal modules total 303 hours.
   > "Total duration of universal practice activities (hours) 303" (Table 2.1 Universal modules: practice activities and suggested duration)
5. The guide does not contain learning materials or case studies.
   > "While it proposes learning activities that often draw from presentations, case scenarios or role play scenario cards, it does not contain the learning materials or case studies." (Creating user-friendly learning materials appropriate to CHWs)
6. CHWs should not be trained in tasks they lack the time or support to perform, with flexibility for emergency crash courses.
   > "In general, CHWs should not be trained in practice activities that they do not reasonably have the time or support to perform; flexibilities may be introduced in circumstances such as “crash courses” to contribute to the response in emergencies." (4. Health policy and health system support for CHW integration in primary health care)
