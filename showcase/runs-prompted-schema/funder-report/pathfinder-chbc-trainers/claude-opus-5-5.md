## Programme approach and training content

The programme's training material is a community home-based care (CHBC) Trainer's Guide produced by Pathfinder International and dated October 2006.

### Basis of the approach

The guide draws on the experience of Pathfinder staff who implemented a CHBC model in Kenya, Tanzania, Ethiopia, Nigeria, and Uganda. It reports that Pathfinder has integrated PMTCT, family planning, and other reproductive health initiatives into its CHBC programmes in recent years. The guide notes that the curriculum is primarily based on Pathfinder's experience in sub-Saharan Africa, but it is intended as a global model that can be adapted to local contexts. According to the guide, the curriculum was pre-tested in Tanzania and Mozambique.

### Model components

The CHBC model described in the guide includes:

- prevention, including Behavior Change Communication (BCC);
- care and training for caregivers of people living with HIV/AIDS, centred on infection prevention;
- community sensitization and mobilization with community leaders and groups;
- two-way referral linkages among CHBC providers and health facilities; and
- support for orphans and vulnerable children.

The guide states that the community health worker's (CHW's) main responsibility should be to transfer nursing and support skills to caregivers.

### Curriculum structure

The stated aim of the curriculum is to provide CHWs with the knowledge and skills needed to deliver CHBC. It consists of a Trainer's Guide and a Handbook for participants. The guide is organised into 13 core units, a practicum, and 5 advanced units.

The core training covers:

- basic nursing;
- prevention and management of opportunistic infections, with guidance on when to refer to a health facility; and
- basic counselling, community mobilization and prevention, and problem solving.

The objectives of the advanced units include:

- distributing nonclinical family planning methods;
- helping prevent mother-to-child transmission; and
- supporting clients on antiretroviral therapy.

### Training methods

The guide describes the training as participatory and based on adult learning principles, with a focus on learning by doing. It recommends that trainings be limited to 15-20 participants. For participants with limited literacy, it suggests that reading and writing activities be replaced or adapted with activities that use pictures or speaking. Three practicums cover skills in client care and community mobilization. The guide also specifies that pre- and post-tests are to be given to all participants.
