## Programme approach and training content

The training material is Pathfinder International's *Community Home-Based Care for People and Communities Affected by HIV/AIDS* Trainer's Guide (October 2006). It states that it aims to give community health workers (CHWs) the knowledge and skills to provide community home-based care (CHBC) to people living with HIV/AIDS (PLWHA) in their communities.

### Programme model

The guide describes Pathfinder's CHBC programmes as involving and building the capacity of local organisations, CHWs and facility-based health care providers. It states that the CHW's main responsibility should be to transfer nursing and support skills to caregivers, who are family or friends of PLWHA, and to PLWHA who are able to do self-care.

### Training content and structure

The Trainer's Guide has 13 core units, a practicum, and 5 advanced units. The advanced units hold additional information that CHWs might find useful after they have covered the core areas. The core training includes basic nursing, prevention and management of opportunistic infections (OIs), guidance on when to refer to a health facility, basic counselling, community mobilisation and prevention, problem solving, and positive living. The guide describes the curriculum as flexible. Units can be combined into a shorter course or a training series, depending on time, funding and the key issues in a particular setting.

### Training methods

The guide states that the training is intended to be participatory and based on adult learning principles. It recommends limiting each training to 15-20 participants, to allow a good trainer-participant ratio and adequate time for skills practice. It also advises that activities requiring reading or writing can be replaced or adapted with activities that use pictures or speaking, because CHWs' literacy levels can vary.

### Practicum and evaluation

The guide provides three practicums covering client care and community mobilisation. Before the home-based practicum, it says PLWHA willing to be cared for by a participant should be identified and informed consent obtained well in advance. It also states that pre- and post-tests are to be given to all participants.

### Development of the curriculum

The guide reports that the curriculum was pre-tested in Tanzania and Mozambique and underwent internal and external peer review.
