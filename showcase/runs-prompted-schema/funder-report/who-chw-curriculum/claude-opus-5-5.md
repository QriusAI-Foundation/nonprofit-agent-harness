## Programme approach and training content

This section describes the programme's training material, the World Health Organization's *Global curriculum guide for community health workers* (2025). It sets out what the guide contains. It does not report on delivery or results.

### Educational approach

The guide adopts the principles and structure of competency-based education (CBE). It describes CBE as oriented to outcome abilities, with less emphasis on time-based training. The guide is designed primarily for formal community health workers (CHWs) who are integrated into the health system and have a structured education pathway, a contract, remuneration and support.

### Structure and content

The guide has three parts:

- **Competencies:** six competency domains containing 25 competencies, which the guide describes as relevant to all CHWs across all contexts.
- **Universal modules:** 12 modules, comprising 11 practice-activity modules and one basic science module. These are relevant to all CHWs, with contextualization. Their suggested total duration is 303 hours.
- **Role-specific modules:** four modules containing 28 topics. Areas covered include:
  - maternal and newborn health
  - HIV and sexually transmitted infections
  - tuberculosis
  - malaria
  - noncommunicable diseases
  - neglected tropical diseases
  - health emergency preparedness and response

The guide states that role-specific content should be selected according to the needs of the population and the scope of practice of CHWs in a given context.

As an example of module design, the home visit module suggests 33 teaching hours. This includes a minimum of 5 hours of shadowing and 6 hours of summative assessment.

### Learning methods and assessment

Suggested learning activities include individual, small group and shadowing experiences. The guide states that teaching should minimize didactic approaches and use practical and authentic learning methods.

Each module outlines both formative and summative assessments. The summative assessment takes place at the end of the module to assess competence to perform the practice activity.

The guide notes that it proposes learning activities but does not contain the learning materials or case studies. It describes itself as not intended to be rigid or prescriptive.

### Supporting conditions

The guide states that maintaining CHWs' knowledge, skills and attitudes depends not only on summative assessment at the end of training. It also depends on continuous training, supportive supervision and follow-up engagement.
