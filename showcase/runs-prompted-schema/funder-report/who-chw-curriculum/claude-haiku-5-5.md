## Programme approach and training content

The programme's training content draws on the WHO *Global curriculum guide for community health workers* (2025). The guide adopts the principles and structure of competency-based education (CBE). Its main audience is CHW educators and those developing CHW curricula. It is designed mainly for CHWs who are formally integrated into the health system after a structured education pathway.

**Structure.** The guide has three parts. The first covers competencies, which are relevant to all CHWs. The guide lists 25 competencies. The second part is the universal modules, which include 11 modules oriented to practice activities and one basic science module. The third part is the role-specific modules. These address service areas including maternal and newborn health, communicable diseases and noncommunicable diseases. The guide states that no CHW can carry out all of the role-specific practice activities, so content must be selected for each context.

**Learning and assessment.** Learning activities are suggested throughout the modules. Module 1, Conducting a home visit, gives a suggested duration of 33 teaching hours and includes a minimum of 5 hours of shadowing a CHW through home visits. The guide describes summative assessments as ones that should be administered at the end of a learning period. Module 12, basic science, should be completed before training on any role-specific activity content.

**Adaptation.** The guide states that tutors are advised to adapt the modules to suit the CHWs they are training. It also states that it is not intended to be interpreted as rigid or prescriptive.

**Dependencies.** The guide is designed to be implemented alongside health system support. It notes that maintaining CHW performance relies on more than summative assessment at completion of training, including continuous training, supportive supervision and follow-up engagement.
