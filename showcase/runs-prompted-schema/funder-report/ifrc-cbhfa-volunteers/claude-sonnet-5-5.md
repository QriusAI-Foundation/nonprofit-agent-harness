## Programme approach and training content

The training material, the IFRC *Volunteer Manual* for Community-based health and first aid in action (CBHFA), sets out a "learning by doing" method: volunteers learn in the classroom and then act in their own communities. The manual states that what volunteers learn responds to the priorities their own community has chosen. It describes community participation as the core of CBHFA. It also states that volunteers are recruited from the communities where they live and work. A facilitator supports volunteers and gives feedback on their learning.

### Module structure

According to the manual, the first three modules are obligatory and give volunteers the background skills to help their community decide its priority concerns.

- **Module 1** covers the volunteer's role and responsibilities in their National Red Cross or Red Crescent Society.
- **Module 2** covers how to involve community members in the learning by doing process.
- **Module 3** includes assessment of the community by volunteers, using tools to map community resources and vulnerabilities.
- **Module 4** covers first aid. It has 20 topics, five of them optional, and teaches volunteers to assess, plan, implement and evaluate first aid for various injuries and illnesses.
- **Module 5** covers major emergencies that may affect a community and how to prepare for and respond to them.
- **Module 6** covers disease prevention and health promotion. Volunteers choose at least five of its 16 topics, depending on the priorities set in the community assessment.
- **Module 7** covers supplementary topics that volunteers may wish to learn in order to provide community education and assistance.

### Communication tools

The manual describes the Community Tools as a set of dialogue cue cards for use with household and community members. It also states that behaviour change communication is most effective when it includes simple messages, delivered clearly and repeated often.

This section describes the content and design of the training material as stated in the manual. It does not report numbers of volunteers trained, communities reached or outcomes achieved.
