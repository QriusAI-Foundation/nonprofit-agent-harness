# Board brief: WHO Global curriculum guide for community health workers (2025)

## What it is
This is a guide from the World Health Organization. It helps people design training for community health workers (CHWs). It is a template, not a ready-made course.

## Key points

1. **Purpose.** The guide helps educators and planners build CHW training. The aim is stronger primary care in communities.

2. **Structure.** It has three parts: competencies, universal modules and role-specific modules. In total there are six competency domains, 12 universal modules and four role-specific modules with 28 topics.

3. **Who it is for.** It is aimed at paid, formal CHWs. The guide says most volunteers cannot do everything in the universal modules.

4. **Pick and choose.** No CHW is expected to learn every role-specific task. Training should match the role and local needs.

5. **Money and support.** Good training needs system support. The guide says this has resource costs that should be covered in national budgets.

6. **Keep it fresh.** Curricula should be reviewed and updated. The guide suggests at least every 5 to 10 years.

## What the board should decide or watch
- **Decide:** whether this guide fits our CHW work. It targets paid, formally supported CHWs. Check this if we rely on volunteers.
- **Decide:** which modules, if any, match our programme's real roles.
- **Watch:** the cost of training, supervision and pay. The guide treats these as part of the package.
- **Watch:** when our training content was last reviewed.

## Evidence cited

1. The guide's purpose is to inform the design and delivery of CHW education and training curricula, to strengthen primary health care and essential services in communities.
   > "The objective of this global competency-based curriculum guide for CHWs is to inform the conceptualization, design and implementation of CHW education and training curricula in order to ultimately strengthen primary health care and delivery of essential services at community level." (1.2 Objective and target audience)
2. The guide is organised into six competency domains, 12 universal modules and four role-specific modules covering 28 topics.
   > "The content is organized into six competency domains, 12 universal modules (11 practice activities plus one module of basic science knowledge), and four role-specific modules, comprising 28 role-specific topics organized under the four modules." (3.1 Overview of the CHW curriculum guide)
3. The guide says most volunteer CHWs, or those working only a few hours a week, cannot carry out all the universal practice activities it expects.
   > "Most volunteer CHWs, or those working only a few hours a week, cannot reasonably carry out all the universal practice activities expected of all CHWs in this framework." (1.2 Objective and target audience)
4. No CHW is expected to carry out all the role-specific tasks, so content must be selected to fit the role and context.
   > "No CHW can carry out all of the practice activities and tasks outlined in the role-specific modules in this framework." (3.1 Overview of the CHW curriculum guide)
5. Integrating CHWs into the health system has considerable resource implications that should be funded through national budgets.
   > "This level of health system integration has considerable resource implications, which should be supported also through inclusion of capital investments and regular recurrent costs in national budgets." (4. Health policy and health system support for CHW integration in primary health care)
6. The guide recommends updating the curriculum regularly, for example at least every 5 to 10 years.
   > "deliver, evaluate and update the new curriculum as regularly as needed (indicated by programme evaluation) or periodically, for example at least every 5–10 years." (3.3 How to use the curriculum guide)
