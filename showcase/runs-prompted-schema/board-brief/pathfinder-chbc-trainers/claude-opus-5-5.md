# Board Brief: CHBC Trainer's Guide (Pathfinder International, 2006)

## What this document is
This is a trainer's guide from Pathfinder International. It is used to train volunteer community health workers (CHWs). These workers care for people living with HIV/AIDS in their own homes and communities. The guide covers basic nursing care, communication, prevention, nutrition, referrals, record keeping and supervised practice.

## Key points

1. **Purpose.** The course gives CHWs the knowledge and skills to provide home-based care to people living with HIV/AIDS in their communities.

2. **Size and structure.** The guide has 13 core units, a practicum and 5 advanced units. A full course is a large commitment of time.

3. **Class size.** Trainings should have no more than 15 to 20 participants. This leaves enough time for skills practice.

4. **Supervision after training.** CHWs need ongoing supervision and support. This helps them handle problems, keep care quality good and keep learning between trainings.

5. **Choosing CHWs.** Depending on local tradition, the community should select CHWs, with help from community-based and faith-based groups.

6. **Client consent.** During practice visits, clients must know they can say no to taking part. Saying no must not affect the care they already receive.

## What the board should decide or watch
- **Capacity:** Can we keep each class to 15 to 20 people?
- **Supervision:** Can we fund and staff ongoing supervision after training ends?
- **Selection:** Will the community choose CHWs, with partner groups helping, as the guide recommends?
- **Consent:** Will practice visits respect clients' right to refuse, with no effect on their care?

## Evidence cited

1. The course gives CHWs the knowledge and skills to provide home-based care to people living with HIV/AIDS in their communities.
   > "This curriculum aims to provide CHWs with the knowledge and skills necessary to provide CHBC to PLWHA in their communities." (Introduction to the Curriculum)
2. The guide has 13 core units, a practicum and 5 advanced units.
   > "The Trainer’s Guide is divided into 13 core units, a practicum, and 5 advanced units that contain additional information that CHWs might find useful after they have covered the core areas." (Introduction to the Curriculum)
3. Trainings should have no more than 15 to 20 participants, which leaves enough time for skills practice.
   > "CHBC trainings should be limited to 15-20 participants to ensure a good trainer-participant ratio and adequate time for skills practice." (Pathfinder’s CHBC Model)
4. CHWs need ongoing supervision and support to handle problems, keep care quality good and keep learning between trainings.
   > "Ongoing supervision and support is needed to help the CHW overcome challenges, to ensure that PLWHA are getting good quality services, and to facilitate learning between trainings." (Pathfinder’s CHBC Model)
5. Depending on local tradition, the community should select CHWs, with help from community-based and faith-based groups.
   > "Depending on the tradition in the region, CHWs should be selected by the community, with the help of CBOs and/or FBOs." (Pathfinder’s CHBC Model)
6. During practice visits, clients must know they can refuse to take part, and refusing must not affect the care they already receive.
   > "Clients should understand that they can refuse to participate in the practicum and that it will not affect the care that they are already receiving." (More About the CHBC Practicum, Evaluation, and Certification)
