# Board brief: CBHFA Volunteer Manual

## What this is
This is a training manual for community volunteers. It covers health, first aid and disaster preparedness. Volunteers learn in a classroom and then act in their own community.

## Key points

1. **A take-home guide.** Each volunteer keeps the manual. It reminds them of what they learned and helps them share it at home and with other households.
   *Board: plan to give every volunteer a copy.*

2. **Some training is required.** The first three modules must be completed. They cover the volunteer role, involving the community, and assessing local needs.
   *Board: make sure new volunteers finish these before other topics.*

3. **The community sets the priorities.** Volunteers and residents assess the area together. They then choose three to five health and safety priorities to work on.
   *Board: expect our work plan to follow these choices. Do not set the topics in advance.*

4. **First aid needs qualified trainers.** Life-saving skills must be taught by first aid trainers that the national society recognises.
   *Board: check that we have access to such trainers before we offer this training.*

5. **Volunteers have rights.** The manual says volunteers are entitled to rewards and to repayment of their out-of-pocket costs.
   *Board: confirm we have a budget and a process for this.*

6. **Reporting goes to the branch.** Volunteers share their reports with branch staff or a supervisor, who give advice and support.
   *Board: name who receives these reports and how often the board sees a summary.*

## What to watch
The manual warns against promising more than we can deliver. When we talk to the community, we should be clear about what this programme can and cannot do. The board should watch for this risk as the programme grows.
