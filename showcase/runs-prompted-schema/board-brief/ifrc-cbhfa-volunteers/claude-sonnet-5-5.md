# Board brief: CBHFA Volunteer Manual (IFRC, March 2009)

## What this is
This is a training manual for volunteers. It comes from the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies. It covers first aid, health promotion, disease prevention and disaster readiness.

## Six key points

1. **How it teaches.** Volunteers learn in a classroom. Then they act in their own community. They bring what they learn back to the classroom to share. The manual calls this the "learning by doing" method.

2. **A long-term commitment.** A National Society that adopts this approach is signing up for long-term health programming. This is not a one-off project. The board should weigh that before taking it on.

3. **Training is built in stages.** The first three modules are obligatory. They give volunteers the background skills to help the community choose its priorities. Later modules are chosen to match those priorities.

4. **Assess before you act.** The manual says volunteers cannot know which projects are right without first assessing the community. The board should expect an assessment step before any activity plan is approved.

5. **Reporting is expected.** A report form should be completed after each household visit or community activity. The board should ask how the organisation will collect and review these reports.

6. **Some skills need qualified trainers.** Basic life support must be taught by qualified first aid trainers recognized by the National Society. The board should check that such trainers are available and budgeted for.

## What the board should decide or watch
- Whether the organisation can sustain a long-term commitment.
- Whether it can fund the training stages, qualified trainers and reporting.
- Whether the community assessment will be done first and properly.

## Evidence cited

1. The manual teaches through a classroom-then-community method called learning by doing.
   > "This is the learning by doing method: learn in the classroom, do in the community." (Introduction)
2. Adopting CBHFA is a long-term commitment for a National Society.
   > "A National Red Cross or Red Crescent Society implementing CBHFA is making a commitment to long-term health programming." (Foreword)
3. The first three modules are obligatory and give background skills for choosing community priorities.
   > "The first three modules of the CBHFA materials are obligatory, and will give you the background skills you need to help your community make decisions about its priority concerns." (Introduction, How CBHFA in action works)
4. A community assessment must come before choosing projects.
   > "Without community assessment, volunteers will not know which future projects or actions are most appropriate." (Module 3, Topic 1: Assessing my community: an overview)
5. A report form should be completed after each household visit or community activity.
   > "A report form should be completed after each household visit or whenever CBHFA activities are performed in the community." (Module 3, Topic 8: Reporting on CBHFA activities in my community)
6. Basic life support skills must be taught by qualified first aid trainers recognized by the National Society.
   > "These skills must be trained by qualified first aid trainers recognized by the National Society." (Module 4, Topic 3: Basic life support (Part 1))
